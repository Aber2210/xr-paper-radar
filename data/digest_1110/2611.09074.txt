Lip synchronization, the task of generating realistic lip movements synchronized with given audio signals, has emerged as a fundamental problem in computer vision and multimedia applications. This technology serves as a cornerstone for various downstream applications, including film dubbing, virtual avatars, video conferencing, and digital content creation. Despite its practical importance and growing demand, achieving high-quality lip-synced video generation remains a challenging problem due to the inherent complexity of modeling the intricate mapping between audio signals and corresponding facial movements.

【实验】
4.1 Experimental Setup
Dataset.
Metrics. 4.1 Experimental Setup
Dataset.
We evaluate on two standard benchmarks: HDTF  [ Zhang et al.(2021b)Zhang, Li, Ding, and
Fan ] , comprising 720p+ talking-face videos, and VoxCeleb2  [ Chung et al.(2018)Chung, Nagrani, and Zisserman ] , comprising in-the-wild celebrity utterances. We randomly select 50 subjects in total, 20 from HDTF and 30 from VoxCeleb2, ensuring diversity in identity, pose, and audio characteristics. We further introduce UniVox-Bench , since prior benchmarks  [ Zhang et al.(2021b)Zhang, Li, Ding, and
Fan , Chung et al.(2018)Chung, Nagrani, and Zisserman , Peng et al.(2025)Peng, Liu, Zhang, Liu, Tang, Wan, Zhang, Liu, and
He ] are restricted to predominantly English speech and/or real human subjects. It comprises 34 subjects, 46% real humans and 54% non-human stylistic characters including cartoon, anime and animals, paired with speech spanning seven languages: English, Chinese, French, Italian, Japanese, Korean, and Spanish. It is collected via web crawling and AI content generator with ElevenLabs  [ ElevenLabs(2024) ] and Gemini  [ Gemini Team, Google(2024) ] .
Metrics.
We evaluate visual fidelity, identity preservation, and lip synchronization. For visual fidelity, we report PSNR and LPIPS for per-frame reconstruction, FID  [ Heusel et al.(2017)Heusel, Ramsauer, Unterthiner, Nessler, and
Hochreiter ] for distributional similarity to real frames, FVD  [ Unterthiner et al.(2018)Unterthiner, Van Steenkiste, Kurach, Marinier,
Michalski, and Gelly ] for temporal coherence, and HyperIQA  [ Su et al.(2020)Su, Yan, Zhu, Zhang, Ge, Sun, and Zhang ] , a no-reference metric that scores perceptual quality without ground truth. Identity preservation is measured by CSIM, the cosine similarity between identity embeddings of the source and generated videos. For lip synchronization, we use LMD, the landmark distance capturing geometric alignment of lip movements, and AVS  [ Yaman et al.(2024b)Yaman, Eyiokur, Bärmann, Ekenel,
and Waibel , Yaman et al.(2024a)Yaman, Eyiokur, Bärmann, Akti,
Ekenel, and Waibel ] , a reference-free score computing the cosine similarity between audio and visual features of a pretrained AV model  [ Shi et al.(2022)Shi, Hsu, Lakhotia, and Mohamed ] . We exclude the SyncNet-based metric LSE-C  [ Prajwal et al.(2020)Prajwal, Mukhopadhyay, Namboodiri, and
Jawahar ] on HDTF and VoxCeleb2 since several baselines train with SyncNet loss, which biases the score in their favor on these datasets.

Emo-LiveSync
Emo-LiveSync: Real-Time Universal Lip Synchronization with Explicit Emotion Control
Sangjoon Yu ∗
Seungeun Lee ∗
JoonHyeok Shin
Ji-Su Kang In this paper, we propose an efficient universal lip-synced video generation method that achieves high-quality lip synchronization without compromising visual fidelity. Leveraging LivePortrait with pre-trained implicit keypoints, we introduce a training strategy that distills knowledge from a pre-trained video-to-motion model into audio-to-implicit-motion mapping, optimized with complementary Charbonnier, velocity, and Wing losses for stable cross-modal alignment. The consistent deformation provided by the warping module enables both high-fidelity results and real-time inference, while naturally generalizing to stylistic non-human characters beyond humans. We further propose a training-free, model-agnostic expressive control gate that explicitly manipulates emotions through parametric activation envelopes, enriching contents without additional supervision. Extensive experiments demonstrate that our approach is both robust and effective for lip synchronization, offering a compelling alternative to computationally expensive diffusion-based methods.
