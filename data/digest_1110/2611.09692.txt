【引言】
 Generating 3D human poses from natural language is a fundamental problem spanning vision, graphics, and embodied AI. Recent progress in text-conditioned generative modeling has advanced text-to-motion synthesis through diffusion and flow-based approaches  [ 37 , 16 , 36 ] . In contrast, text-to-pose generation  [ 8 , 15 , 23 ] remains comparatively underexplored despite posing a distinct challenge: unlike motion generation, which benefits from strong temporal continuity priors, single-frame pose generation must model a highly multimodal distribution over articulated 3D configurations from language alone. This challenge becomes harder when extending from coarse body posture to whole-body articulation with expressive hand poses.
Current text-to-pose methods exhibit two limitations. First, most approaches model joint rotations in Euclidean parameterizations such as axis-angle or continuous 6D rotations  [ 53 ] , and optimize Euclidean regression objectives. However, articulated pose lies on a product manifold of rotation groups rather than a flat vector space. As a result, linear interpolation and Euclidean velocity regression produce optimization targets that are not aligned with the geodesic structure of rotations, particularly for large joint rotations and high-dimensional whole-body configurations. Recent work on geometry-aware articulated modeling  [ 18 , 30 ] suggests that respecting manifold structure can improve generative learning, but these ideas have not yet been explored in text-conditioned single-frame pose generation.
Second, existing text-to-pose systems largely ignore detailed hand articulation. Most prior methods operate on body-only skeletons with 22 joints, even when datasets are stored in richer SMPL-H or SMPL-X formats  [ 27 , 34 , 32 , 8 , 9 ] . Recent LLM-based generators  [ 15 , 23 ] similarly omit finger degrees of freedom. Yet hand articulation carries a substantial fraction of pose semantics in natural language descriptions, including gestures such as pointing, grasping, waving, or open-palm interactions. Modeling full SMPL-H articulation therefore requires learning coherent dependencies across 52 coupled rotations spanning body posture

We evaluate PoseRieFlow along two complementary axes. We first report results on the standard body-only benchmark used by prior text-to-pose work, where all baselines are applicable (Section  4.2 ). We then evaluate full SMPL-H whole-body generation including articulated hands (Section  4.3 ), the primary target setting of PoseRieFlow. In both regimes we isolate the effect of the proposed manifold formulation through matched-compute comparisons between Riemannian and Euclidean flow matching, and we further analyze inference efficiency, guidance, tokenization, conditioning, and training data in Section  4.4 .
4.1 Experimental Setup
Data.
We train and evaluate on PoseScript-A2H2, the union of the PoseScript automatic (A2) and human-written (H2) caption splits, which pairs SMPL-H poses from AMASS with natural-language descriptions. Our default models are trained on PoseScript-A2H2 alone, matching the data available to the PoseScript VAE. A training-data ablation (Section  4.4 ) additionally trains on a 300k-pose subset of Motion-X++  [ 51 ] , a whole-body motion dataset whose frame-level captions provide detailed hand articulation that PoseScript does not emphasise. Baseline results use the authors’ publicly released pretrained models. All methods are evaluated identically on the test split ( N = 19990 N=19990 ).
Metrics.
All generated and reference poses are first rotated about the vertical axis to a common heading. We measure text-pose alignment with the PoseScript retrieval model [ 8 ] and report R@1 in a 32-candidate gallery, the correct item plus 31 seeded distractors, with identical galleries for all methods, a chance level of 3.125 % 3.125\,\% , and exact ties counted as misses, in both the text-to-pose (T2P) and pose-to-text (P2T) directions, together with Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) in the retrieval model’s pose-embedding space as a measure of distributional realism. We report FID in the whole-body setting (Section  4.3 ), where it is computed on complete 52-joint poses. In the body-only projection, zeroing the hand joints degenerates the retrieval feature covariance and renders the FID estimate unstable, so we omit it there. We additionally report per-joint kinematic error against the reference pose: Procrustes-aligned mean per-joint position error (PA-MPJPE) in millimetres and mean geodesic rotation error (Geo) in degrees, where Geo ∖ 0 \mathrm{Geo}_{\setminus 0} denotes the same error with the global orientation excluded. In the whole-body setting we further report Geo restricted to the 30 finger joints and fingertip PA-MPJPE summed over the ten fingertips (TipPA). Stochastic methods are sampled with ten seeds per caption and reported as mean ± \pm std, and their diversity (Div) is the mean pairwise PA-MPJPE between multiple samples, averaged over the test set.
Baselines.
We compare against the PoseScript conditional VAE [ 8 ] , the recent LLM-based generators ChatPose  [ 15 ] , PoseLLaVA  [ 14 ] , and UniPose  [ 23 ] . PoseRieFlow is reported in two variants that share architecture, parameter count, and training budget and differ only in the flow formulation: Ours (Riem.) , the Riemannian model with geodesic interpolation and tangent-space targets, and Ours (Eucl.) , the Euclidean ablation that interpolates and regresses in 6 6 D. 【结论/局限】
 We presented PoseRieFlow , a Riemannian flow matching model for text-conditioned whole-body pose generation. By formulating the flow on the product manifold SO ​ ( 3 ) 52 \mathrm{SO}(3)^{52} with constant-velocity geodesic targets, training a compact Diffusion Transformer with AdaLN time conditioning and cross-attention to Flan-T5-Base features, we outperform far larger LLM-based baselines on kinematic fidelity while modelling the full SMPL-H articulation. Ablations show that the Riemannian formulation consistently improves rotational and joint-position fidelity over Euclidean flow matching at matched compute across inference budgets and guidance weights, that token-level cross-attention rather than encoder capacity drives text alignment, and that part tokenization improves kinematic fidelity while cutting attention cost relative to per-joint tokenization. and future work.
