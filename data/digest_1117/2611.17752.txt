【引言】
 Novel view synthesis (NVS) aims to recover the geometry and appearance of a scene from a limited set of images and render views from camera poses that were not observed during training. It has been used in areas such as virtual reality, autonomous driving, and visual effects. Neural radiance fields (NeRFs)   [ 27 , 28 , 3 , 1 ] are able to reconstruct detailed scenes, but their rendering process relies on sampling points along camera rays, which makes inference relatively expensive. 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)   [ 20 ] uses anisotropic Gaussian primitives to represent a scene and performs differentiable rasterization, offering a practical compromise between rendering quality and computational cost.
Under sparse-view supervision, limited geometric constraints make 3DGS susceptible to overfitting, producing blurred regions, floating artifacts, or distorted structures at unseen viewpoints   [ 4 ] . Existing approaches incorporate depth or geometric priors   [ 38 , 6 ] , image- or video-diffusion guidance   [ 37 , 43 ] , and modifications to Gaussian representations   [ 5 , 9 ] . Dropout-based methods randomly deactivate Gaussians during training   [ 33 , 31 , 39 ] , while structured masking strategies further consider spatial anchors, paired consistency, or depth reliability   [ 10 , 22 , 23 ] . However, the lineage relationships formed during densification remain less explored in dropout design.
Dropout changes the Gaussian subsets participating in rendering, reducing dependence on fixed combinations and potentially improving generalization. However, neighboring Gaussians may compensate for dropped primitives, weakening the effect of independent dropout. We define a lineage family as an initialized Gaussian, termed the founder, together with its descendants generated through cloning or splitting. Members of the same family often exhibit spatial clustering and contribute to nearby regions. Independent dropout may therefore leave many related members active, limiting its effect on the family’s overall contribution.
Based on these observations, we propose Stage-wise Lineage-aware Dropout (SLD), which applies shared masks to the descendants of selected families during the structure-regularization stage. Because family-level deactivation may interfere with late-stage detail optimization, SLD subsequently switches to independent Gaussian dropout. 3.4 Lineage-Balanced Densification
We introduce Lineage-Balanced Densification (LBD) to complement SLD by redistributing densification opportunities according to family-level gradient scores and descendant counts. LBD regulates growth concentration without enforcing equal family sizes, while preserving the cloning and splitting quotas determined by the original 3DGS criteria.
Fixed Budgets and Expanded Candidate Pool. At each densification step, LBD computes the screen-space positional gradients and applies the original 3DGS gradient threshold θ g \theta_{g} together with the operation-specific criteria to construct separate candidate sets for cloning and splitting, denoted by 𝒫 t \text ​ c ​ l ​ o ​ n ​ e \mathcal{P}_{t}^{\text{clone}} and 𝒫 t \text ​ s ​ p ​ l ​ i ​ t \mathcal{P}_{t}^{\text{split}} , respectively. 4.5 Cross-Framework Validation Studies
The individual and joint effects of SLD and LBD are reported in Table  4 . The results show that both SLD and LBD improve the reconstruction quality, with SLD yielding a notable gain when used alone. The combination of SLD and LBD yields further improvements, indicating that the two modules provide complementary benefits during training. SLD controls which Gaussians participate in rendering, while LBD distributes densification opportunities among different lineage families.
The effects of individual dropout and family-shared dropout under different founder policies are shown in Table  5 . When enabled, protection retains founders during pruning and splitting in Stages 1 and 2 and excludes them from dropout in Stage 2; it is disabled in Stage 3. With individual dropout, founder preservation improves SSIM but reduces PSNR, leaving LPIPS unchanged. With family-shared dropout, it improves all three metrics and yields the best overall performance, supporting the default SLD configuration.
Table  6 shows only small performance differences across t s ∈ { 5000 , 7000 , 9000 } t_{s}\in\{5000,7000,9000\} , with all other settings fixed. Although t s = 9000 t_{s}=9000 achieves the highest PSNR, we adopt t s = 7000 t_{s}=7000 , which yields the best SSIM and LPIPS.
4.5 Cross-Framework Validation
We also test the adaptability of the proposed method by adding the lineage-aware modules to FSGS  [ 44 ] , CoR-GS  [ 42 ] , DNGaussian  [ 24 ] and Scaffold-GS  [ 25 ] . After the integration, PSNR and SSIM increase, and LPIPS decreases in all four frameworks, as shown in Table  7 . These results indicate that SLD and LBD can be introduced into different sparse-view Gaussian Splatting pipelines, while the extent of improvement depends on the specific framework and evaluation metric.

Address:  Joint International Research Laboratory of Spatio-temporal Information and Intelligent Location Services, Guilin 541004, China We present a lineage-aware framework for sparse-view 3D Gaussian Splatting that combines Stage-wise Lineage-aware Dropout (SLD) with Lineage-Balanced Densification (LBD). SLD applies family-shared dropout before switching to independent dropout, while LBD redistributes densification opportunities among families without changing the original cloning and splitting quotas. Lineage statistics and ablation studies support the use of family information for regularization, with SLD providing the larger gain and LBD offering additional improvements. Quantitative and qualitative comparisons show competitive performance, although the gains vary across datasets, metrics, and training frameworks.
