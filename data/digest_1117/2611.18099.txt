【引言】
 Feed-forward networks now recover 3D scenes directly from images  [ 54 , 24 , 51 ] . Given a video, the Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer (VGGT)  [ 51 ] predicts the camera pose and dense geometry of every frame in one forward pass, and later models such as π 3 \pi^{3}   [ 56 ] , VGGT- Ω \Omega   [ 52 ] , and Depth Anything 3 (DA3)  [ 26 ] work the same way. Their global blocks make this possible, since cross-frame self-attention lets each view draw on all other views.
We study how such a model can reconstruct every frame of a long video in one coordinate frame at a constant cost per frame. Long videos challenge these models in two ways. First, the cost of cross-frame attention grows quadratically with the number of frames, and its memory grows proportionally with every frame. A two-thousand-frame video at 518 × \times 392 resolution requires > > 80 GB GPU memory, so long videos should be split into chunks or subsampled, and subsampling leaves most frames unreconstructed. Second, accuracy suffers even when the video can fit in memory. Our evaluations show that, at 500 frames, the full VGGT keeps its trajectory error within a 0.2 m tolerance in only 70% of 50 ScanNet scenes  [ 10 ] . Its predicted confidence flags failures barely better than chance ( Fig.   1 c), so users cannot tell which results to trust.
Most existing remedies approximate attention over all frames within a budget, and each of them gives up part of this goal. Streaming variants  [ 65 , 66 , 62 ] process each frame once as it arrives and freeze its tokens, so early errors persist. They keep at most half the scenes within tolerance ( Tab.   1 ), and InfiniteVGGT misplaces walls in two long videos ( Fig.   5 ). Chunked pipelines  [ 14 , 16 ] see one window at a time, so their chain of windows drifts wherever no loop closure corrects it. Token merging  [ 39 ] keeps every frame in one pass and runs out of memory in most scenes at 2,000 frames. Methods that give each frame a few keys take them at fixed intervals  [ 46 , 12 , 47 ] or select them by content, such as diverse views  [ 63 , 37 ] or retrieved frames  [ 66 ] . Two questions remain open: whether a long video needs attention over all its frames, and whether the keys of each frame must depend on the video content.
We hypothesize that the keys of a long video can be chosen without looking at its content. One shared context of a few dozen frames, spread evenly over the video, can serve as the keys of every frame, and the other frames need not read one another. 3.2 The alignment band
On three 100-frame sequences of 7-Scenes  [ 40 ] , the median divergence of VGGT stays near or below 1 up to global block 10, peaks in block 14, and falls below 1 at block 17, a profile that π 3 \pi^{3} shares ( Fig.   3 a). On 31 spread frames per ScanNet scene, we correlate the attention share of two distant frames (at least four pinned frames apart) with their co-visibility, the overlap of the surfaces that they observe. For VGGT, the rank correlation stays at or below 0.16 in blocks 0 to 9 and reaches 0.66 to 0.68 in blocks 13 to 15 ( Fig.   3 b), so these blocks relate frames by content. We call global blocks 10 to 15, where this correlation rises and peaks, the alignment band and call the later blocks the readout blocks.

Metrics and future work
EcoVGGT inherits its backbone’s failures in large rooms, since a backbone trained on short clips aligns distant views poorly ( Sec.   3.6 ). On π 3 \pi^{3} , its relative pose errors exceed those of the full model by 4% to 11%, as 31 keys give each frame fewer views, and 125 keys cut this gap to about 1%. Trained on ScanNet alone, refocusing raises the trajectory error of EcoVGGT (VGGT) on the synthetic NRGBD and Replica and narrows the predicted field of view on Replica by 4.5° toward that of ScanNet, so future work will train the band on varied cameras. Indoors, spread keys cover what every frame sees, whereas a car on KITTI rarely returns to a place, so most keys share no content with the current frame. Without loop closure, EcoVGGT ( π 3 \pi^{3} ) drifts online to a mean error of 164 m, against 65 m for the streaming-trained LongStream  [ 8 ] . Recent keys lower it to 106 m, and future work will select keys by co-visibility and add loop closure.
Our results show that feed-forward 3D models can scale to long indoor videos through a small shared context, a design that future models can adopt in training.
