【引言】
 Depth imaging supports a broad range of applications including 3D reconstruction for autonomous navigation  [ 1 , 2 ] and consumer electronics  [ 3 ] . Among
depth-sensing technologies, single-photon LiDAR (SPL) is attractive for its exceptional temporal resolution and single-photon sensitivity  [ 4 , 5 ] , which enable accurate ranging even at long range and under low illumination. Cost-effective lightweight SPL sensors however, provide only a small number of pixel waveforms; typically 8 × 8 8\times 8 or 16 × 16 16\times 16   [ 6 , 7 ] . This severe spatial limitation restricts their practical use and motivates computational super-resolution (SR) approaches to recover a high-resolution depth map from such measurements.
Fig. 1: Reconstruction quality (RMSE in mm) vs. time (sec.) of our proposed method in comparison to DELTAR  [ 8 ] , DepthAnything-V2  [ 9 ] , DVSR  [ 10 ] , and Marigold-DC  [ 11 ] evaluated on 12 clean scenes of Middlebury dataset.
To compensate for the low spatial resolution, sensor-fusion methods use a co-registered high-resolution (HR) intensity or reflectivity image to guide the upsampling of low-resolution (LR) LiDAR data  [ 12 , 13 , 10 , 14 , 15 , 16 , 17 , 18 , 19 ] . These methods span statistical reconstruction  [ 20 , 21 , 22 ] , learning-based feature fusion  [ 12 , 13 , 6 , 10 ] , and, more recently, to generative models using conditional diffusion  [ 19 ] . Although these approaches improve reconstruction quality, plug-and-play methods  [ 6 ] and iterative diffusion  [ 19 , 11 ] incur substantial inference costs (Figure  1 ). Recovering detailed depth from a single frame becomes particularly challenging when the LiDAR provides only 8 × 8 8\times 8 waveforms (Figure  2 ). Earlier approaches exploit multiple frames from a video sequence  [ 6 , 10 ] or operate at a higher base spatial resolution  [ 19 ] , leaving efficient reconstruction from a single frame at such low spatial resolution insufficiently explored.
We address both obstacles with a framework built on amortized variational distillation. We first learn a multi-step conditional diffusion teacher over HR depth maps and then distil this distribution into a one-step generator. Following the amortized formulation of  [ 23 ] , a data-fidelity term is integrated to enforce consistency of the reconstruction with the observations. We re-render the predicted HR depth back into a LR ToF histogram and penalise its deviation from the measured histogram. III-F Inference We aim to recover a × 128 128\!\times\!128 depth map from an × × K 8\!\times\!8\!\times\!K SPAD measurement and a × 128 128\!\times\!128 reflectivity guidance, which is severely ill-posed: the sensor provides only 64 64 spatial waveforms, and we aim to recover the missing spatial structure. Our key observation is that a faithful depth map reconstruction must satisfy two requirements simultaneously: it must (i) be consistent with the corresponding histogram when passed through the same forward model, and (ii) supply a high-frequency spatial structure that is consistent with the high-resolution reflectivity guidance.
In addition, we focus on improving computational efficiency to support practical deployment of low-cost SPAD-based sensing platforms (such as smartphones, or robotic sensors) in the future.
We meet these requirements as follows.
We address the extreme single-photon depth super-resolution problem with a two-stage framework.
In Stage 1 we learn a histogram-conditioned diffusion prior over high-resolution depth maps ( III-C ) (see Figure  3 ). In Stage 2 we distil this multi-step prior into a single-step amortized posterior sampler ( III-E ) (see Figure  4 ); crucially, the amortized formulation allows us to incorporate a differentiable data-consistency operator derived from the single-photon forward model, which is what grounds the generator in the measured waveforms. The result is a single forward pass that reconstructs a × 128 128\!\times\!128 depth map in approximately 83 83  ms, while providing much better accuracy than recent monocular depth estimation methods.

degrades sharply as the reflectivity is corrupted, since its RMSE rising from 33.0 33.0 to 61.2 61.2  mm, whereas every variant that

Second, the two visual conditionings are complementary and provide robustness. On all scenarios, the full
𝐇 + 𝐝 DA + 𝐑 \mathbf{H}{+}\mathbf{d}_{\mathrm{DA}}{+}\mathbf{R} variant has the lowest RMSE. Under noise, the full model retains the lowest RMSE ( 20.16 20.16 and 21.75 21.75 ). The results confirm that both signals are needed for the best overall trade-off. Removing either 𝐝 DA \mathbf{d}_{\mathrm{DA}} or 𝐑 \mathbf{R} produces more error under noisy conditions compared to the full variant.
V-B Effect of ℒ C \mathcal{L}_{C}
The data-consistency term ℒ C \mathcal{L}_{C} is applied only during training. To verify that the reconstruction agrees with the measurement at test time, we take the depth map produced by each method, re-render it into a low-resolution histogram, and compare it with the measured histogram at clean case.
Tables  IV and  V show the effect of the consistency loss in our framework. Including ℒ C \mathcal{L}_{C} reduces mean depth RMSE across all four simulated conditions. When only the reflectivity guidance is degraded ( Noise 1 ), RMSE decreases from 21.26 21.26 to 20.16 20.16  mm using ℒ C \mathcal{L}_{C} , although training uses only clean reflectivity images. Our approach distils a multi-step conditional diffusion teacher into a single-step generator using an amortized variational distillation framework. A differentiable measurement-constrained data-consistency term further encourages the reconstructed depth to reproduce the measured low-resolution ToF histogram under the forward model. To address the severe
ill-posedness of the reconstruction problem, we combine the ToF waveform with a high-resolution reflectivity image and an unaligned monocular relative-depth estimate, providing complementary metric and spatial guidance.
Our experiments demonstrate that the proposed approach outperforms the evaluated baselines across both clean and degraded measurement conditions, while the ablation studies demonstrate the benefits of the multimodal conditioning and histogram data-consistency term. Qualitative experiments on real 8 × 8 8\times 8 SPAD measurements further demonstrate transfer from simulated training data to real sensor captures under both well-lit and low-light conditions.
