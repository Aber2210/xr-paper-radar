【引言】
 Monocular depth estimation is a core perception capability for autonomous driving and robotics. Supervised methods rely on costly LiDAR or manual annotation, whereas self-supervised methods recover depth from monocular video via photometric consistency alone [ 17 , 63 ] . The pursuit of benchmark accuracy has driven a steady increase in model scale: transformer-based [ 42 , 61 ] and diffusion-based self-supervised methods [ 44 , 25 , 18 ] routinely exceed tens of millions of parameters while pushing the state of the art. However, as perception tasks increasingly shift toward resource-constrained edge devices, deploying networks at such scale poses considerable challenges. Consequently, compact architectures have emerged as an active research direction [ 59 , 58 , 66 ] .

Existing research on lightweight monocular depth estimation focuses primarily on network architecture design [ 32 , 66 , 12 , 57 ] . For instance, Lyu et al. [ 37 ] introduced high-resolution skip connections to preserve spatial details; Zhang et al. [ 59 ] proposed a lightweight CNN-Transformer hybrid; and more recently, Chen et al. [ 4 ] introduced feature purification to improve estimation efficiency. However, despite continual architectural innovation, lightweight monocular depth estimation has long faced an unexplained accuracy ceiling: the most competitive compact models cluster within a narrow accuracy band, and pushing beyond it typically demands more parameters or more data [ 32 , 66 , 59 , 12 , 57 ] .
In our experiments, we observe that pretraining does not always substantially improve the performance of lightweight networks—a phenomenon consistent with that reported by Muralidharan et al. [ 39 ] in supervised tasks. The prevailing view holds that this issue can only be resolved by further scaling up the network [ 66 ] or by introducing additional semantic segmentation datasets [ 48 ] ; yet both remedies run counter to the original intent of lightweight design and severely constrain the development of lightweight monocular depth estimation. We argue that the root cause might lie in the training pipeline inherited from networks of conventional scale: pretrain first, then fine-tune directly [ 15 , 14 ] . 4.4 Complexity and Runtime
Table  3 reports model complexity and speed at 640 × \times 192 on a MacBook Air (M2) and an iPhone 12 (A14), measured with Apple Core ML (Float32) and averaged over 200 runs after 50 warm-ups. ReForgeDepth attains the highest FPS on both devices, sustaining real-time inference on the iPhone 12 with the best accuracy–efficiency trade-off.
Why FLOPs fail to predict on-device latency. On mobile SoCs, latency depends more on operator-level hardware support than on FLOPs. Although Monodepth2-Res18 [ 17 ] requires 2.2 × \times more FLOPs than ReForgeDepth, both achieve nearly identical latency on the iPhone 12 because Monodepth2-Res18’s convolution-only architecture is well optimized by mobile inference engines. Conversely, PuriLight [ 4 ] has modest FLOPs but runs significantly slower because its frequency-domain operations are poorly supported on mobile devices.

Overall, SS has enhanced the depth accuracy of compact models without increasing the inference burden. The longer the self-supervised training time of the traditional training strategy, the more limited the improvement achieved. These results indicate that the traditional training strategy may not be able to achieve the performance that compact models can reach, while SS reduces the mismatch between training strategy and model capacity and enables small models to better utilize their existing expressive capabilities.

Email:  lizhang@hfut.edu.cn This paper reveals the mismatch between the training strategy and model capacity in lightweight self-supervised depth estimation. We propose “Signal Scaffolding”—a three-phase strategy that gradually adjusts the training signals to approach the target task, thereby unlocking the latent representational capacity of the lightweight model without the need for additional dataset or parameters. In addition, we design ReForgeDepth, which reduces the number of parameters while retaining detailed information and the receptive field. On the KITTI and Cityscapes datasets, ReForgeDepth achieves the best balance of accuracy and efficiency among the compared methods, and outperforms other models of the same scale by a large margin.
Two limitations remain: photometric consistency degrades depth around moving objects, and the lack of ground-truth depth caps accuracy. Future work will integrate motion-aware masking into Signal Scaffolding and explore stronger geometric priors in even more compact architectures, narrowing the supervised gap while preserving on-device efficiency.
