【引言】
 The research community has witnessed revolutionary progress driven by data-centric approaches, enabling the rapid advancement of large language models (LLMs) [ 7 , 10 , 12 , 44 , 16 ] and vision-language models (VLMs) [ 43 , 2 , 29 ] . Tremendous efforts have been invested in reproducing this success in embodied AI, leading to rapid advances in vision-language-action models (VLAs) [ 6 , 22 , 54 , 5 , 4 ] and world-action models (WAMs) [ 65 , 30 , 66 ] . More recently, inspired by LLM agents that harness external tools, agentic robot systems  [ 1 , 17 ] have been developed to orchestrate multiple execution modules for long-horizon tasks.
Much of this progress remains fundamentally task-centric, where robots execute given instructions while humans remain peripheral to the formulation. This paradigm is insufficient for robots intended to serve, assist, and collaborate with people in real-world scenarios, where humans are not merely obstacles to avoid, but active participants whose behaviors, intentions, and requests continuously shape the task. We therefore argue that embodied AI should increasingly embrace a user-centric perspective, placing situated human context at the core of robot decision-making and execution .
Studying this new paradigm in the data-driven era requires datasets that capture not only robot sensory and action signals, but also how humans perceive, communicate, and interact with robots throughout task execution. Existing Human–Robot Interaction (HRI) datasets  [ 46 , 64 ] , however, are largely collected in controlled tabletop settings and focus on short-horizon interactions, providing limited coverage of situated human behaviors. Meanwhile, egocentric vision has been increasingly explored for robot learning  [ 60 , 13 ] , yet limited effort has studied how intentional cues from the human perspective can complement robot observations during HRI.
To bridge this gap, we introduce HEIR, H arnessing E gocentric I ntent for Human– R obot Interactions, a dataset for studying user-centric human–robot interaction during long-horizon tasks in naturalistic household environments. As illustrated in Fig.  1 , HEIR pairs human egocentric video, measured gaze, and speech with robot observations and teleoperated navigation–manipulation trajectories. These recordings capture the human and robot context surrounding each request, including referents visible only from the human perspective, while each continuous session preserves both multi-stage execution and state changes across requests. 5.2 Subtask Planning
We evaluate subtask progression offline using recorded robot observations, annotated intent, and annotated execution history, comparing each planner before and after fine-tuning on HEIR. As shown in Tab.  4 , pre-trained planners struggle to judge subtask progression from robot observations: Cosmos3-Nano predicts Continue for nearly every query and never switches to a correct next subtask. Fine-tuning on HEIR substantially improves all three models, raising decision accuracy by 25–37 points and next-subtask type accuracy by 25–55 points. The fine-tuned Qwen3.5-4B has the strongest overall planning result, reaching 61.06% Core and 36.85% Completion F1. The effect of the intent source on initial-subtask planning is analyzed in Appendix  E.3 .

5.4 Robot Manipulation
We report robot manipulation performance for several prevailing VLA/WAM models after fine-tuning on HEIR, including DreamZero  [ 65 ] , π 0 \pi_{0} -FAST  [ 40 ] , π 0 \pi_{0}   [ 5 ] , π 0.5 \pi_{0.5}   [ 18 ] , FastWAM  [ 66 ] , and Lingbot-VLA2  [ 63 ] . We found that existing open-source checkpoints exhibit limited zero-shot generalization to our naturalistic household scenarios, often leading to severe execution failures and making direct evaluation uninformative. We therefore focus on fine-tuned model performance. As shown in Tab.  6 , stronger recent models generally reduce both joint-space and end-effector prediction errors, with Lingbot-VLA2 achieving the best overall performance across first-step and action-chunk prediction metrics.
In real-robot tests on 20 pick-and-place tasks with three trials each, Lingbot-VLA2, FastWAM, and π 0.5 \pi_{0.5} achieve success rates of 68.33%, 60.00%, and 36.67%, respectively, following the same ranking as offline action prediction. This suggests that our offline evaluation can serve as an efficient proxy for comparing manipulation models before costly real-robot deployment.
5.5 Real-Robot Experiments
We deploy the selected models from the four preceding tasks in a closed-loop system and evaluate their end-to-end performance on HEIR. We adopt Lingbot-VLA2 for manipulation based on the real-robot comparison above and its native torso control (Appendix  C.4 ). For navigation, InternVLA-N1 achieves a subtask-level success rate of 81.25 % across 32 evaluated navigation subtasks. 5.1 Human Intent Understanding
Here, we use Gemma-4-31B  [ 52 ] as the base model, with additional experiments on Qwen3.8-27B  [ 42 ] , Muse-Glimmer-30B  [ 33 ] , and InternVL3.5-38B  [ 59 ] reported in the Appendix  E . At a similar model scale, Gemma-4 slightly outperforms Qwen3.8 and substantially outperforms InternVL3.5 on human intent understanding; it outperforms Muse-Glimmer mainly in plan prediction. As these large pretrained models already achieve strong performance without task-specific training, we focus our analysis on the contributions of human-centric intentional cues and interaction history.

The instruction-only baseline achieves the lowest accuracy, indicating that intent parsing is non-trivial and cannot be directly solved from language priors alone. Adding the robot view significantly improves performance, and further adding the human egocentric view brings additional gains. And overlaying gaze on the egocentric frames provides further gains, particularly compared with using a center prior as a proxy for attention. Notably, combining all modalities yields the best overall performance.

B.5 3D Point Cloud and Trajectory Comparison In this paper, we introduce HEIR, a dataset and benchmark for studying user-centric human–robot interaction in long-horizon, naturalistic household environments. HEIR jointly captures human egocentric video, gaze, and instruction together with robot observations and execution trajectories, with structured annotations linking human intent to planning and execution. We formulate a hierarchical evaluation spanning human intent understanding, subtask planning, navigation, and manipulation, and benchmark representative models through both offline and real-robot experiments. Our results demonstrate the value of harnessing human-centric intentional cues for natural HRI, while also revealing substantial error accumulation when these components are integrated for long-horizon execution. We believe HEIR provides an important empirical foundation for developing embodied AI systems that place humans at the center of robot understanding, planning, and interaction.
