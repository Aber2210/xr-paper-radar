3.3.2 Avatar Performance
Vidu S2 Avatar (81.71 Overall) and LiveTalk 1.3B (75.85 Overall) lead avatar performance with distinct metric profiles ( table   3 ). Vidu S2 Avatar achieves the highest scores in A–V Sync (99.60), Stability (97.25), Identity (86.12), and Following (36.90), paired with 63.68 in Quality. LiveTalk 1.3B leads in Quality (71.27) and reaches 16.59 in Following, while recording 86.44 in A–V Sync, 96.06 in Stability, and 83.89 in Identity. AptAvatar 14B ranks third at 70.37 Overall, ahead of Quark Live Avatar 14B at 62.83 and SoulX-LiveAct 18B at 53.51; all seven systems support a custom avatar and receive the + 5 +5 bonus.
Middle-ranking systems also differ in temporal stability and visual quality despite similar lip synchronization and identity preservation scores. AptAvatar 14B and Quark Live Avatar 14B tie at 98.89 in A–V Sync and score similarly in Identity (81.33 vs. 79.75), but differ in Stability (78.06 vs. 55.30) and Quality (55.55 vs. 43.51). 4.2 Benchmark Coverage roster.
The evaluation protocol specifies a roster of 300 test cases: 60 per task, with 12 at each duration tier and three independent runs per case and model. The results reported in this paper cover these 300 cases ( section   3.1 ). All five duration strata receive equal aggregation weight within each task, so longer sessions contribute equally to the final assessment rather than in proportion to their duration.
2.4 Comparison with Existing Benchmarks
Table   1 positions StreamEval against existing video generation and interactive benchmarks across task coverage and evaluation horizons. Avatar-performance benchmarks assess rendered speakers, including visual quality and speech-motion synchronization, while Full-Duplex-Bench evaluates spoken dialogue without video output. VideoFDB additionally evaluates full-duplex conversational perception and audiovisual response generation [ 44 ] . Its naturally occurring nonverbal cues complement our authored speech-input dialogue and prescribed-performance tasks; our broader task coverage does not imply coverage of its user-video perception requirements. For video editing, WBench and WBench-Editing focus on world-model environments, while EditVerseBench covers instruction- and reference-conditioned editing of external videos [ 68 , 36 , 29 ] . WBench-Editing also reports a separate reference-conditioned comparison. Task interfaces and control timing jointly define the interaction protocol.

7 Contributions and Acknowledgments StreamEval brings five interactive streaming video tasks into a unified benchmark of 300 scripted test cases, linking evolving requests to explicit execution and state-preservation requirements. Our evaluation of 37 models reveals complementary strengths across tasks, while online action following and reference-based editing remain challenging. In reference editing, the highest reported Edit Success score is 15.00 on a 0–100 scale. Improving request execution alongside visual fidelity and state continuity is therefore an important direction for future model design. A natural next step is to collect human pairwise judgments and examine their agreement with the task-specific evaluation rubric.
