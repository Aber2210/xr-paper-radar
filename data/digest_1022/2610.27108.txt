3.2 Average Contribution Criterion We build on StopThePop  [ 20 ] , a state-of-the-art Gaussian Splatting renderer whose exact per-pixel depth ordering removes popping artifacts and keeps occlusion boundaries intact.
Hybrid transparency  [ 10 ] , in contrast, sorts only the front-most Gaussians and composites all remaining ones without enforcing occlusion ordering.
Leaving even fully occluded Gaussians with a nonzero contribution stands in opposition to our motivation of occlusion-aware densification.
However, our method is not dependent on the per-pixel sorting of StopThePop: it relies only on the per-pixel blending weights that virtually every Gaussian Splatting rasterizer already computes.
Our contributions can therefore transfer to other bases (evaluated on 2DGS  [ 12 ] and 3DGS  [ 13 ] in Sec. A.2 of the supplementary material).

3.1 Occlusion-Awareness
As discussed in Sec.   2.1 , densification-related statistics are gathered via a visibility filter based on a view-frustum test.
This reflects potential, not actual, observations of a Gaussian.
From here on, we refer to Gaussians that are not rendered at all as occluded :
transmittance saturates before reaching them at every pixel they cover.
With a frustum-based filter, occluded Gaussians thus enter the accumulated densification statistics despite not participating in the rendered image.
Although their gradients are zero, every occluded view still increases the criterion’s observation count and updates the maximum-radius tracking.
Increasing the observation count results in a downward pressure on the densification criterion of any Gaussian that lies within the frustum while being obstructed.

4.1 Datasets and Metrics 4.1 Datasets and Metrics
We evaluate on the common Gaussian Splatting scenes: Mip-NeRF360  [ 1 ] (9 scenes), Tanks & Temples  [ 16 ] (2 scenes), and Deep Blending  [ 11 ] (2 scenes).
Additionally, we evaluate on Zip-NeRF  [ 2 ] , whose 4 large apartment scenes (alameda, berlin, london, nyc) exhibit heavy occlusions between rooms, with an occlusion rate of 0.63 0.63 against 0.15 0.15 – 0.48 0.48 on the other datasets (definition and per-scene values in Sec. D). Zip-NeRF is the established stress test for this regime: prior work reports 3DGS under-densification and over-smoothing on these scenes despite extensive tuning  [ 5 , 19 ] .
For Zip-NeRF, we use images at half their original resolution, mirroring common 3DGS target resolutions, and hold out every 8th image as the validation set, following the standard protocol  [ 13 ] .
We report peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR), structural similarity (SSIM)  [ 25 ] , learned perceptual image patch similarity (LPIPS; VGG backbone)  [ 30 ] , the final number of Gaussians, and the training time.
Large-scale scenes are prone to over-smoothing due to under-densification  [ 19 , 5 ] .
We therefore also report the gradient magnitude similarity deviation (GMSD)  [ 27 ] , a metric specifically sensitive to blur.
We read these metrics jointly:
perceptual ones such as LPIPS credit reconstructed detail that e.g . PSNR penalizes when not pixel-aligned.
Likewise, a lower Gaussian count is only better at comparable quality:
a starved model is not more efficient.
