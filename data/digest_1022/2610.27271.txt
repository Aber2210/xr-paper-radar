VIII Conclusion
XoNav demonstrates how a hip exoskeleton can serve as a
personalized, gait-informed haptic interface that conveys navigation
guidance directly through joint motion rather than symbolic
audio or vibration. Its four actuators render Advance,
two-level Braking and Steering cues across the sagittal and
frontal hip planes, coordinated with wearer-initiated movement
through a classifier using only two thigh IMUs. The classifier
achieved 95.2–97.9% leave-one-subject-out recall, with gait
transitions detected within tens of milliseconds, enabling
gait-dependent cue delivery and suppression during standing.
Constrained human-in-the-loop optimization yielded reliably
distinguishable Braking cues with minimal resistance and
individually comfortable Steering cues across ten participants,
with maximum Stop stiffness remaining below 53% of the
safety bound. This personalization allowed guidance without
unnecessarily restraining natural walking. In 150 blindfolded
Wizard-of-Oz trials, participants maintained 98.8% corridor
adherence within a 0.75 m passage while improving progression
speed by an estimated 4.9% per trial. The same cues also
supported autonomous navigation using a body-worn camera,
RTAB-Map and Nav2, including detours around unmapped
obstacles. Together, these findings demonstrate the potential
of personalized kinesthetic guidance for assisted navigation,
although validation remains limited to blindfolded sighted
participants in controlled indoor environments. Future work
will evaluate XoNav with people with visual impairment
against white-cane navigation, improve device wearability
and extend autonomy to less structured environments.

control architecture and haptic cues, alongside blindfolded
