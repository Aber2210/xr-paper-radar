✓ \checkmark CityPaint generates city-scale textured 3D scenes from geographic coordinates and a text prompt. Instead of synthesizing urban environments from scratch, we exploit appearance priors from pre-trained 2D diffusion models and cast city texturing as a geometry-grounded generative reconstruction problem. As shown in Fig.  2 and Fig.  3 , the pipeline contains four stages: scene initialization, global iterative generation, terrain refinement, and building refinement.

In Stage 1, we construct a 3D city mesh from OpenStreetMap (OSM) data and establish heightfield-aware camera trajectories for stable observation. In Stage 2, a conditional diffusion model iteratively synthesizes the scene guided by rendered depth and semantic maps. The generated views are accumulated into a global UV atlas using a face-wise top- K K camera baking strategy to suppress artifacts from grazing angles. 4.1 Experimental Details 4.1. Experimental Details
Across all three stages (global generation, terrain refinement, and building refinement), we adopt the open-source generative model Z-Image  ( Cai et al., 2025 ) as the shared diffusion prior.
In the global stage, we iteratively texture the scene onto an 8192 × 8192 8192\times 8192 UV atlas from 320 views. The cameras follow our heightfield-aware trajectory with 8 scan strips and 40 views per strip, using a 48 ∘ 48^{\circ} downward elevation and a 40 ∘ 40^{\circ} field of view. For each selected view, we render a depth map, a semantic control map, and the current partial appearance from the UV atlas, and run 25 denoising steps in the conditional diffusion model.
For the subsequent refinement stages, terrain refinement operates on overlapping 1024 × 1024 1024\times 1024 UV tiles with a denoising strength of 0.2 and the shared global-noise initialization described in Eq.  15 . Building refinement uses object-centric orbit cameras sampled across azimuths at elevations of 20 ∘ 20^{\circ} and 35 ∘ 35^{\circ} to enhance facade details, and updates the atlas through the normal-aware selection rule in Eq.  21 with at most two views per face. All experiments are conducted on a single NVIDIA A100 GPU (40 GB).

4.4 Ablation Study Metrics
A key challenge in evaluating city-scale 3D generation is the lack of reliable reference image distributions. Existing works commonly use Google Earth imagery  ( Xie et al., 2024 ; Xie et al., 2025 ) as an evaluation benchmark. However, such imagery is not ideal for assessing photorealism because it often contains reconstruction artifacts, compression noise, and noticeable blurring, as illustrated in Fig.  4 . Consequently, metrics computed solely on this distribution may not accurately reflect the visual quality of generated results.
To address this limitation, we introduce a complementary high-quality benchmark, termed CityWeb . We collect a large-scale set of real-world urban photographs from publicly accessible web sources and apply a multi-stage filtering pipeline to remove non-photographic, low-resolution, and watermarked images. A GPT-based scoring mechanism  ( Achiam et al., 2023 ) then ranks the candidates according to urban relevance, visual clarity, and overall fidelity. The top 20,000 images are retained to form the CityWeb benchmark, yielding a cleaner and more realistic reference
