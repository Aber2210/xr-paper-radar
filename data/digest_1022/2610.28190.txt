Given an unposed sequence of RGB images { I i } i = 0 N − 1 \{I_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N-1} with known intrinsic matrix K K , our goal is to recover the camera trajectory { T i ∈ SE ( 3 ) } i = 0 N − 1 \{T_{i}\in\mathrm{SE}(3)\}_{i=0}^{N-1} and subsequently reconstruct a global 3D Gaussian scene representation 𝒢 = { ( 𝝁 j , 𝚺 j , α j , 𝐟 j ) } j = 1 M \mathcal{G}=\{(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{j},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j},\alpha_{j},\mathbf{f}_{j})\}_{j=1}^{M}   [ 16 ] . Differentiable rasterization renders a synthesized RGB image ℛ ⁡ ( 𝒢 , T i , K ) \mathcal{R}(\mathcal{G},T_{i};K) along with an accumulated opacity map A ⁡ ( 𝒢 , T i , K ) A(\mathcal{G},T_{i};K) .
To reconcile local tracking with global geometric consistency and suppress cumulative drift, CrossFit-GS introduces an observation-isolated pipeline under a deferred-commitment protocol (Fig.  2 ). Rather than continuously updating a shared global map during pose estimation, our framework decouples trajectory solving from global scene synthesis through three functional components: (i) Alternating Split and Local Tracking , which streamwise partitions incoming frames into interleaved temporal folds and derives initial pose proposals; (ii) Cross-fit Refinement and Validation , which optimizes target camera poses against the frozen opposite submap while updating the activated owner submap only upon passing validation controls; and (iii) Final Reconstruction under Deferred Commitment , which discards intermediate submaps once all camera extrinsics are verified and frozen, utilizing the fixed poses to train a unified global 3DGS representation from scratch.

4.1 Experimental Setup
Datasets.
Following previous benchmark evaluation protocols [ 44 , 15 , 9 , 33 ] , we evaluate on Tanks and Temples  [ 17 ] using a frame-subsampling stride of 5 to simulate challenging wide-baseline camera motion. Additionally, we benchmark on CO3D-V2  [ 30 ] across representative scenes featuring diverse geometric and textural complexities. In line with standard evaluation setups, every 8th frame is held out as test views for novel view synthesis.
Metrics.
Novel view synthesis is evaluated using PSNR, SSIM  [ 42 ] , and LPIPS  [ 50 ] . Camera trajectory estimation is aligned via Sim ⁡ ( 3 ) \mathrm{Sim}(3) Umeyama alignment and evaluated using Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) as well as Relative Pose Errors for translation ( RPE t \text{RPE}_{t} ) and rotation ( RPE r \text{RPE}_{r} )  [ 20 , 2 ] .

By enforcing strict observation isolation and refining each candidate pose against a frozen out-of-fold submap, our approach effectively eliminates the target-to-map self-explanation loop and prevents pose errors from being absorbed into Gaussian primitives. Experiments on benchmarks demonstrate that CrossFit-GS achieves substantial improvements in both global trajectory accuracy and novel-view rendering. Furthermore, our diagnostic studies confirm that isolating target-frame observations during pose refinement is crucial for preventing trajectory drift and reconstructing geometrically faithful 3D representations.
