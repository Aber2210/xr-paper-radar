5.5 Application: time-to-quality in streaming We consider the continuous level of detail setting for 3DGS.
Given a posed training set 𝒟 = { ( I i , Π i ) } i = 1 M \mathcal{D}=\{(I_{i},\Pi_{i})\}_{i=1}^{M} , the goal is to learn a single Gaussian representation 𝒢 = { g j } j = 1 N \mathcal{G}=\{g_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N} that can be rendered under a variable primitive budget τ ∈ ( 0 , 1 ] \tau\in(0,1] , such that
𝒢 τ ⊆ 𝒢 \mathcal{G}_{\tau}\subseteq\mathcal{G} with | 𝒢 τ | = ⌈ τ ​ N ⌉ |\mathcal{G}_{\tau}|=\lceil\tau N\rceil .
At every budgeted representation, our aim is that 𝒢 τ \mathcal{G}_{\tau} remains spatially coherent and degrades gracefully as τ \tau decreases.
Following our pilot study (Sec.  3 ) which demonstrates that image complexity is a surrogate of the reconstruction error, our aim is to benefit from the image complexity signal, readily accessible at training time. Specifically, we apply two simple modifications to the optimization process of state-of-the-art 3DGS-MCMC  [ 8 ] . First, we utilize complexity to foster (re)allocation of the Gaussians in regions with higher complexity (Sec.  4.1 ).

A 3DGS representation encodes a scene as a set of Gaussian primitives, where each Gaussian g j = ( μ j , α j , Σ j , 𝐜 j ) g_{j}=(\mu_{j},\alpha_{j},\Sigma_{j},\mathbf{c}_{j}) is parameterized by its position μ j \mu_{j} , opacity α j \alpha_{j} , covariance Σ j \Sigma_{j} , and color coefficients 𝐜 j \mathbf{c}_{j} . To expose complexity to the optimization process, we augment each primitive with a scalar variable C j C_{j} , which stores the local image complexity associated with the current Gaussian. This relates to the complexity field in Eq.  2 , but differs from the post-hoc analysis in our pilot study since, during training, Gaussian positions and supports change, and therefore their complexity values must be updated online.

In practice, 3DGS variants rely on opacity as a proxy for this error signal: during optimization new primitives are concentrated where opacity has accumulated, and regions the optimizer has not yet resolved remain under-served precisely because they have not been resolved. B.2 Method
Scope of the LoD Formulation. We studied how to allocate Gaussian primitives as rendering budgets shrink in continuous LoD splatting. Our pilot study showed that image complexity, accessible before any optimization, predicts spatial reconstruction error in 2D and 3D, yet trained models fail to allocate capacity accordingly in a form that a shrinking budget preserves. Building on these observations, we design a method allocating primitives based on complexity which determines their survival at inference, using a variance objective to maintain uniform error across scales. Evaluated on three datasets, our approach matches the strongest baselines at full capacity, while achieving the highest integrated quality across budgets, and degrades more gracefully, with the largest gains below 1 % 1\% capacity and faster time-to-quality in streaming.
