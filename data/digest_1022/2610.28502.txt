2 Related works
Gaze-guided VLMs.
GazeLLM uses gaze-centered cropping  ( Rekimoto, 2025 ) , while Voila-A learns gaze-conditioned visual representations  ( Yan et al., 2024 ) . GazeVLM combines global/local image preprocessing with gaze-guided token selection after encoding  ( Yoon et al., 2025 ) . It preserves surrounding evidence through sampled context tokens, but its image-based reduction does not select video frames or condition token allocation on the question. GazeLens instead selects frames before encoding and blends focused and complementary features according to the question within two tokens per frame. StreamGaze  ( Lee et al., 2025 ) evaluates this balance through Past, Present, and Proactive questions.
Token reduction and frame selection.
FastV and PyramidDrop prune visual tokens within the language model  ( Chen et al., 2024b ; Xing et al., 2024 ) , whereas LLaMA-VID produces a compact representation before language-model input  ( Li et al., 2024 ) . For video, LongVU and FrameFusion also exploit temporal redundancy in visual features  ( Shen et al., 2024 ; Fu et al., 2024 ) . Frame-Voyager and M-LLM Frame Selector learn to choose frames using question relevance and model-derived supervision  ( Yu et al., 2024 ; Hu et al., 2025 ) . Across these approaches, the stage of reduction determines which computation can be avoided. We therefore place frame selection before learned encoding and use gaze to allocate the retained spatial-token budget.
Streaming MLLMs.
VideoLLM-online, ViSpeak, Dispider, Flash-VStream, and LiveVLM address interaction with arriving video  ( Chen et al., 2024a ; Fu et al., 2025 ; Qian et al., 2025 ; Zhang et al., 2024 ; Ning et al., 2025 ) , with StreamingBench evaluating streaming comprehension  ( Lin et al., 2024 ) . Our study concerns the visual input budget for each QA example. Here, frame selection controls the amount of encoded video, and gaze-conditioned pooling controls its spatial representation. Ngan Le In this paper, we proposed GazeLens for compact egocentric video question answering. First, adaptive frame selection uses HOG transitions and image sharpness to reduce temporal redundancy before visual encoding. Next, gaze-focused and complementary pooling preserve evidence within and beyond fixation, while a question-conditioned gate adjusts their contribution. A global summary then retains scene context alongside the primary token, yielding two visual tokens per selected frame. Together, these modules address the need to preserve relevant evidence within a limited visual budget.
