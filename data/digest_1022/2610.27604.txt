3.1 Hierarchical Spatiotemporal Encoder
Sparse monocular videos contain complementary appearance and geometry evidence
with different spatial granularities and semantic abstraction levels. Fine-grained
appearance cues are important for recovering local details and object boundaries,
while geometry-aware features provide structural information across observations.
To capture these complementary properties, we introduce a Hierarchical
Spatiotemporal Encoder (HSE) that integrates geometry and appearance branches
with different granularities. HSE further employs Temporal-Projective
Rotary Positional Encoding (TP-RoPE) to establish spatiotemporal correspondence
among visual tokens based on their image locations, observation timestamps, and
camera geometry.

【结论/局限】
 We presented HiQGS, a feed-forward framework for reconstructing
renderable Gaussian scene states from sparse posed monocular video
frames. The key idea is to separate the organization of context
observations from the decoding of a requested scene state.
HiQGS directly predicts a target-specific Gaussian field without
prescribing a motion model or enforcing primitive correspondence across
time, and the encoded context can be reused to decode multiple target
timestamps. Experiments on both dynamic and static reconstruction
benchmarks demonstrate strong rendering and geometric accuracy,
supporting hierarchical context encoding and temporally grounded
querying as an effective design for generalizable feed-forward Gaussian
reconstruction. .
