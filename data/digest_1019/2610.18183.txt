2.4 Analysis 2.1 Study Design
Participants put on an AR headset in an object-rich (cluttered) office environment and completed a sequence of eight 45-second trials during which they were tasked to spot and select objects from a list displayed on one of the office walls.
In each trial, participants completed a standing selection task from a fixed spot in the office. They were presented with a virtual list of 16 objects and must sequentially find and select, by pointing and clicking with the controller, as many objects as possible in the mixed reality office scene within 45 seconds. There were 8 trials total. Participants completed a surprise memory recall task after the 4th trial, were subsequently instructed to create a mental model of their environment through a verbal cue, and finally completed the cued memory recall task after the 8th trial.
2.1.1 Variables
We examined a number of independent variables in this study, including object-, user- and context-level factors.
Our object-level factors of interest were the virtuality of an object [objects were either physical or virtual] and the size of the object [large, medium, small, very small]. Of the 32 objects in the scene in each trial, 16 were physical, and participants were tested for recall of 8 physical and 8 virtual objects at the end of their trials. The remaining physical and virtual objects were included in the primary selection task, to ensure that participants were not biased to recall of certain objects due to the selection task alone. The objects were also distributed across a range of sizes, though we could not achieve a perfectly balanced design with regards to the size variable, due to the physical constraints of the office-themed space.
The user-level factors we were interested in were cueing (user alerted to the recall task) and dual task (additional audio task in half the trials). We define cueing as a verbal cue directing participants to remember objects in the scene. All participants complete the memory recall task after the first 4 trials without prior knowledge (no cue condition). Participants are then verbally cued (cue condition) to remember all objects in the scene, and complete another 4 trials in the environment. We use a secondary audio response task (participant pressing a controller button when they hear a certain radio alphabet letter) that goes on in conjunction with the selection task as a means of increasing cognitive load.
