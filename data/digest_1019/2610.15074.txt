5.4 Measures and Data Analysis and Findings As shown in Table 2 , the Semantic Understanding Agent (SUA) achieved consistently high performance across object identity (97.78%), part labeling (96.90%), and semantic direction-frame grounding (99.26%). The near-perfect direction-frame accuracy indicates that MotionAnimator can robustly establish functional object directions despite variations in asset structure and imported orientation, while the remaining part-labeling errors primarily involved visually or structurally similar components.
A clearer gap emerged in the Motion Planning Agent (MPA), which achieved 99.16% MPF1 but 87.77% MAA, indicating that identifying “ which parts should move ” was substantially easier than grounding “ how they should move ”. Motion-axis errors were most pronounced for Aerial Mobility (75.00%) and Articulated & Sliding Objects (70.00%), whereas Rotating Appliances achieved 100.00% on both metrics. Human judgments of the complete motion plans showed a similar pattern, with an overall Intent-to-Motion Alignment (IMA) of 5.83 ± 1.16 5.83\pm 1.16 and the lowest rating for Aerial Mobility ( 5.30 ± 1.40 5.30\pm 1.40 ). Inter-rater reliability was good for IMA, with five-rater average-measure absolute agreement of I ​ C ​ C ​ ( 2 , 5 ) = .794 ICC(2,5)=.794 . Overall, these results suggest that MotionAnimator reliably identifies object semantics and behaviorally relevant parts, while precise motion-axis and articulation grounding remains the primary challenge for geometrically complex behaviors.
4.4 Code and Runtime Realization Evaluation
To evaluate TQ4 , we evaluated both the generated Unity C# code and the resulting runtime animations from two complementary perspectives.
(i) Human Animation Evaluation :
We recruited five independent human raters, including two VR developers, two instructors, and one student, to evaluate the generated animations. For each test case, raters viewed the animation from three complementary viewpoints and assessed four dimensions on a 7-point scale:
OLM ( Object-Level Motion ) measures the correctness of the object’s overall motion;
PLM ( Part-Level Motion ) measures whether the required parts move appropriately;
MD ( Motion Direction ) measures whether the motion directions or axes are correct; and
OPC ( Object–Part Coordination ) measures whether object- and part-level motions are properly coordinated.
