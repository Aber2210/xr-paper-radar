6.1 Baseline Methods
We compare three representative models  ( Shafir et al., 2024 ; Barquero et al., 2024 ; Li et al., 2025 ) in long-term motion generation as our baselines. To adapt them to our specific task setting, we apply minimal necessary modifications. For a fair comparison, all models utilize CLIP as their text encoder. Under the Anaphoric Text-to-Motion (A2M) setting, the text condition for each baseline is modified to take the weighted sum of the current segment’s text embedding and all preceding segments’ text embeddings. This modification explicitly injects historical context into the current generation step, enabling the models to maintain awareness of previously established constraints without requiring complex architectural redesigns. All baselines are carefully retrained to accommodate this contextual input setting.

5.4 Metrics settings: independent vs. anaphoric instructions
To systematically analyze how models handle cross-segment dependencies, we consider two instruction formats:
T2M (independent Text-to-Motion): The standard setting where text instructions are strictly independent. Each segment description is self-contained without referencing prior states.
A2M (Anaphoric Text-to-Motion): Our proposed continuous setting where instructions exhibit anaphoric compositions. The model must resolve cross-dependencies and maintain constraints established in preceding segments.
Using these formats, we report three configurations to isolate the effect of ELMA supervision:
(1) T2M Train → \rightarrow T2M Inference serves as the standard baseline, evaluating the model’s original generative capacity without complex dependencies,
(2) T2M Train → \rightarrow A2M Inference evaluates the zero-shot generalization of standard models when subjected to anaphoric instructions, exposing their inherent limitations in state propagation,
and (3) A2M Train → \rightarrow A2M Inference evaluates models after fine-tuning on our ELMA training set, demonstrating their acquired capacity to explicitly learn and preserve anaphoric constraints.
5.4 Metrics
To evaluate motion quality, we report four widely used metrics. FID measures the Fréchet distance between the distribution of generated motions and that of the ground-truth (GT) motions. R-Precision@3 indicates how highly the generated motions are ranked with respect to the corresponding GT descriptions through retrieval within a mini-batch. In addition, Matching Score computes the distance between text and motion embedding vectors to evaluate their alignment. Finally, Diversity measures the variance of the generated motion features across all sequences, reflecting the model’s ability to produce varied movements.
