An overview of the proposed method is shown in Fig.  2 . We propose a factor-graph based design that takes IMU measurements, up-to-scale poses from GFM predictions, and relative scale measurements derived between consecutive predictions in order to create a natively metric scale trajectory and 3D reconstruction. GFMs such as MapAnything  [ 18 ] or DA3  [ 19 ] have a degree of real-world scale by training on datasets with known scale — however this merely improves their predictive capability and predictions are not constrained by physical measurements. Our method recovers real-world scale — as demonstrated in Fig. 3 — using IMU sensor measurements and is compatible with sensors typically used on drones, phones or AR glasses.
III-A Notation and preliminaries
We denote single object vectors or matrices in bold (e.g. image I as an array of RGB values) and sets of objects in calligraphic font (e.g., set of images ℐ \mathcal{I} ). A batch consists of a set of frames, where each frame corresponds to an input image I , pose T , depth map D , and confidence map C .
In the results section we use models from Depth Anything 3 (DA3)  [ 19 ] and VGGT- Ω \Omega   [ 26 ] though any model that jointly infers predictions from a set of input images can be used  [ 17 , 18 ] . From model predictions, per-frame geometry can be obtained by backprojecting the depth map D with the camera intrinsics obtained either from a prior calibration or directly from model predictions.
III-B Visual-inertial initialisation
We begin each sequence with a visual-inertial initialisation phase aided by GFM predictions, similar to MASt3R-Fusion  [ 5 ] . Specifically, we adapt the visual-inertial initialisation method from VINS-Mono  [ 27 ] . This module uses Structure-from-Motion (SfM) to estimate the gyroscope biases and the gravity vector by aligning IMU preintegration to the SfM poses.

We evaluate both the trajectory and reconstruction quality of FoundationVIO across three datasets.
We first compare FoundationVIO’s pose estimation accuracy with respect to both classical and GFM-enabled methods. Results are presented both with the system configured in odometry mode and also in SLAM mode with loop-closures enabled.
We then evaluate FoundationVIO’s dense 3D reconstructions against other state-of-the-art GFM-enabled SLAM methods. Finally, we evaluate the real-time performance of our method. All experiments are carried out on a laptop equipped with an Intel Core(TM) i7-13850HX and an RTX 3500 Ada Generation Laptop GPU with 12 GB VRAM for equal comparison.
IV-A Datasets
The three datasets consist of 1) a self-collected indoor dataset, 2) the Oxford Spires dataset  [ 32 ] , and 3) the ScaRF-SLAM dataset  [ 33 ] . Together, these datasets span different indoor and outdoor scenes and cover a wide range of sensor rigs and environments.
The Aria Office dataset was self-collected using the Meta Aria glasses (Gen 1) which is equipped with two large-FoV greyscale cameras (HFoV = 150 ∘ \,=150^{\circ} ), a front-facing RGB camera (FoV = 110 ∘ \,=110^{\circ} , 15 Hz at 1408 x 1408 resolution), and two IMUs (1 kHz, 800 Hz). The sequences consist of head-worn trajectories touring several floors of an office environment in multiple loops and pass through narrow corridors, low-texture staircases, and office rooms.
