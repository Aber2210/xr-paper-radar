3.3 Simultaneous Localization and Mapping
Problem setting and dataset.
We evaluate SLAM on all seven indoor scenes of
7-Scenes  ( Shotton et al., 2013 ) , where the objective is to estimate the
camera trajectory while reconstructing the environment from an incoming
video stream. Each sequence contains 17–33 s of continuous video,
providing long trajectories for evaluating accumulated drift and
revisit-driven correction.

3.2 Multi-view Depth Estimation and 3D Point-Cloud Reconstruction We evaluate all competitive baselines with and without SMoRe across five
downstream tasks: camera pose estimation, multi-view depth estimation,
3D point-cloud reconstruction, simultaneous localization and mapping
(SLAM), and novel view synthesis. We perform three runs per experiment
and aggregate results across runs. For each task, we describe the problem
setting and datasets, followed by the compared methods, metrics, and . A Gaussian decoder converts the stored geometry into
rendered images. The datasets and evaluation splits are described in
Appendix  H.5 .
I.1 Source Views and Geometry
We render each held-out target from eight nearby input keyframes.
Source views are selected using the sum of camera-centre distance and
viewing-direction angle, computed from the ground-truth cameras.
The target itself is excluded. Baseline and + SMoRe +\textsc{SMoRe}{} use identical
source views.
Each source keyframe retains the depth, confidence, and camera intrinsics
predicted when it first enters a window. Its depth is unprojected into
3D points in that window’s local coordinate system
(Appendix  C ). Baseline places these points in the global
map using chained window transformations T k T_{k} . SMoRe uses the window
transformations obtained after online revision. We fit a similarity
transformation between the estimated and ground-truth input cameras.
We use this alignment to express the target camera in each map’s
coordinate system.
Before decoding, we centre the source points at the centre of the source
cameras and normalize their scale by the median source depth. We discard
the lowest-confidence 30% of points in each frame. We also remove points
at depth discontinuities, defined by a relative depth difference above
5% between neighbouring pixels. Both systems use the same filtering
procedure. Images are rendered at 384 × 288 384\times 288 resolution with a 4:3
aspect ratio. Portrait CO3Dv2 sequences retain their original orientation.
I.2 Gaussian Decoder and Training
We use a Gaussian decoder to predict rendering parameters from the
source images and their geometry. Each source pixel provides eleven
input channels:
