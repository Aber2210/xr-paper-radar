Fixed-Budget Methods.
Following ConeGS  [ 1 ] , we evaluate a set of primitive budgets { 100 ​ k , 500 ​ k , 1 ​ M , 2 ​ M } \left\{\mathrm{100k},\mathrm{500k},\mathrm{1M},\mathrm{2M}\right\} and average the metrics over the 24 scenes (Figure  6 ).
Table  2 and supplementary Tables  S.1 – S.4 break the results down by dataset and scene.
We treat differences below 0.05 dB PSNR or 0.002 SSIM or LPIPS as on par.
Our error-driven densification helps most where primitives are scarce.
It is better than its prior-free baseline 3DGS-MCMC in all three metrics at every budget, by 0.84 dB PSNR at 100 ​ k \mathrm{100k} and 0.34 dB at 2 ​ M \mathrm{2M} .
On the outdoor Mip-NeRF 360 scenes, most of this gain lies in the background (Table  1 ): from 500 ​ k \mathrm{500k} on, our PSNR there improves by about 1 dB, against at most 0.23 dB in the foreground.
When inspecting the distribution of blended primitives for low-budget models (cf. Figure  2 ), we find our primitives are not as concentrated on the foreground as those in 3DGS-MCMC.
Prior-based methods show clear benefits for smaller budgets, but their lead over 3DGS-MCMC shrinks as the budget grows.
ConeGS is 1.05 dB ahead of 3DGS-MCMC in PSNR at 100 ​ k \mathrm{100k} , whereas at 2 ​ M \mathrm{2M} , 3DGS-MCMC is on par with ConeGS and ahead of EDGS.
Our method preserves the steady improvement of 3DGS-MCMC with higher budgets on top of its established low-budget advantage.
From 500 ​ k \mathrm{500k} on, it has the highest PSNR and SSIM of all fixed-budget methods, and its PSNR lead over the second-best method grows from 0.18 dB at 500 ​ k \mathrm{500k} to 0.34 dB at 2 ​ M \mathrm{2M} .

We keep what makes 3DGS-MCMC robust, its fixed budget and gradual growth, and replace the two decisions it bases on opacity: the image error, weighted by a learned confidence, decides where new primitives go, and their contribution to the rendered images decides which primitives make room for them.
This simple change improves on 3DGS-MCMC at every budget we test.
