2.3. Training Objective and Optimization
The overall training objective combines rendering loss, geometric objective, and camera pose regularization. We optimize training under sparse views by combining early stopping and motion-aware dropout to stabilize convergence and improve efficiency.
Rendering Loss
Let 𝐈 ^ \hat{\mathbf{I}} be the rendered image and 𝐈 gt \mathbf{I}_{\text{gt}} the ground truth. We adopt a standard combination of ℓ 1 \ell_{1} and SSIM:
(6)
ℒ render = λ 1 ​ ‖ 𝐈 ^ − 𝐈 gt ‖ 1 + λ SSIM ​ ( 1 − SSIM ​ ( 𝐈 ^ , 𝐈 gt ) ) . \displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{render}}=\lambda_{1}\,\|\hat{\mathbf{I}}-\mathbf{I}_{\text{gt}}\|_{1}+\lambda_{\text{SSIM}}\,\big(1-\text{SSIM}(\hat{\mathbf{I}},\mathbf{I}_{\text{gt}})\big).
Geometric Objective
The geometric objective ℒ geo \mathcal{L}_{\text{geo}} combines photometric consistency with depth and flow regularization, as detailed in Section  2.1.1 .
Camera Regularization
The camera regularization term ℒ cam \mathcal{L}_{\text{cam}} constrains the deviation of optimized camera poses from their initial values, preventing excessive pose modifications.
Overall Objective
The complete training objective is a weighted sum of all three components:
(7)
ℒ total = λ render ​ ℒ render + λ geo ​ ℒ geo + λ cam ​ ℒ cam , \displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}}=\lambda_{\text{render}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{render}}+\lambda_{\text{geo}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{geo}}+\lambda_{\text{cam}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{cam}},
where λ render \lambda_{\text{render}} , λ geo \lambda_{\text{geo}} , and λ cam \lambda_{\text{cam}} are the weights for the rendering loss, geometric objective, and camera regularization, respectively.

2. Methodology
Our overall algorithm is illustrated in Figure 2 .
Following the deep learning perspective outlined in the introduction, we organize our approach into two complementary categories: Supervision Enhancement and Training Regularization . This unified framework addresses the three fundamental causes of overfitting in sparse-view 3DGS: noisy data (corresponding to camera miscalibration), insufficient data, and excessive model capacity. In the following subsections, we detail each component.
2.1. Supervision Enhancement
To address insufficient supervision and noisy data in sparse-view 3DGS, we enhance the training data through two strategies: pseudo-view synthesis addresses insufficient data, while joint camera–scene optimization addresses noisy data.
2.1.1. Optical Flow Guided Pseudo-view Synthesis
Given a predicted depth map D D with intrinsics ( f x , f y , c x , c y ) (f_{x},f_{y},c_{x},c_{y}) , we synthesize a nearby view by applying a small in-plane translation 𝒕 = ( t x , t y , 0 ) ⊤ \boldsymbol{t}=(t_{x},t_{y},0)^{\top} . The induced pixel shifts are
