【实验】
 Experimental Setup. SENRI-Agent is built upon two representative persistent memory methods, WorldMM and MAGIC-Video by reusing their constructed memories and retrieval
interfaces. We evaluate SENRI-Agent on SENRI-Bench against the original workflows of the two
baselines using two inference models, Qwen3.8-27B and Gemini 3.7 Flash. The
maximum number of reasoning rounds is set to 5, followed by one answer round,
as in the baselines. We keep 3 reports and 4 evidence frames per report in the working memory and set maximum 3 checks per verification.
Main Results. Table  3 shows that
SENRI-Agent improves all four configurations using the same stored memories
and retrieval interfaces. With Gemini 3.7 Flash, WorldMM improves from
58.8% to 62.6% and MAGIC-Video from 58.6% to 61.8%. With Qwen3.8-27B,
they improve from 47.6% to 50.4% and from 45.2% to 47.8%, respectively.
The overall gains range from 2.6 to 3.8 percentage points, with improvements
on both ES and SE across all four configurations. This supports the benefit
of targeted verification and evidence retention across the tested systems.

D Limitations and Future Work
Benchmark scope.
Comparing ES and SE. In this work, we introduced SENRI, a new perspective on long video understanding that focuses on the nexus between events and their spatial contexts. We instantiated this problem in SENRI-Bench, a benchmark of long, uninterrupted, self-recorded egocentric videos with six task types spanning two complementary directions, Event → \rightarrow Space and Space → \rightarrow Event. Through an extensive evaluation of direct perception, evidence seeking, and persistent memory approaches, we found that existing systems still struggle on this task. Our analysis further revealed two representative limitations of current memory systems.
