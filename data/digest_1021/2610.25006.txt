IV-A Task and Data Collection
A bimanual peg-in-hole task - widely regarded as one of the most challenging benchmarks in robotic assembly due to its high precision requirements - is selected for the experimental validation. As shown in Fig. 5 , both the enclosure and the connector are placed at random locations on the table within the robot’s workspace. The objective is for the robot to grasp both objects and precisely insert the connector into the enclosure with a 1 mm tolerance (Fig. 6 ). The task must be completed without dropping either object, demanding highly coordinated and fine-grained bimanual manipulation. To fully evaluate our system and the proposed TEC strategy, three data collection modalities are compared. The first modality follows Conventional Telemanipulation (CT), consistent with prior works [ 9 , 2 , 8 ] , in which the operator fully controls both robot arms and EEs. This mode serves as our experimental baseline. In this setting, the grasping pose varies across episodes, leading to inconsistent initial conditions for the insertion phase. The second modality HT incorporates vision-based path planning for automated grasping, followed by full telemanipulation to complete the insertion. The third modality applies the TEC strategy, in which grasping is performed autonomously and the operator only provides fine adjustments during the insertion phase. For both HT and TEC modalities, only demonstrations after the autonomous grasping stage are collected.
