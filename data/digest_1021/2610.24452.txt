Given a language query, HumanSkill-4D selects the described region of an avatar and, for a 4D query, every matching interval in its driving pose sequence. As shown in Fig.  2 , the framework prepares reusable memories offline, then uses a frozen MLLM Φ MLLM \Phi_{\mathrm{MLLM}} to interpret queries online.
In the offline phase, we build a semantic memory ℳ semantic \mathcal{M}_{\text{semantic}} that links body-part and garment categories to the avatar’s Gaussians through category scores p i ​ c p_{ic} registered from a frozen 2D human parser Φ parse \Phi_{\mathrm{parse}} . The memory also stores category-level appearance descriptions generated by Φ MLLM \Phi_{\mathrm{MLLM}} from multi-view images (Sec.  4.2 ). A motion memory ℳ motion \mathcal{M}_{\text{motion}} stores joint and root trajectories for each driving pose sequence. A registry ℛ \mathcal{R} shared across avatars provides reusable templates for checking pose and motion conditions, along with temporal relations that specify how these conditions overlap or follow one another in time (Sec.  4.3 ).
In the online phase, Φ MLLM \Phi_{\mathrm{MLLM}} reads the query q q and the category names and descriptions to select target categories 𝒞 q \mathcal{C}_{q} . Their stored scores determine the spatial mask 𝐬 q \mathbf{s}_{q} ( Where ), which is the complete output for a 3D query. For a 4D query, the model also reads the registry’s text cards and composes operators into a motion program Π q \Pi_{q} . A deterministic executor runs this program on ℳ motion \mathcal{M}_{\text{motion}} to obtain the temporal mask 𝐡 q \mathbf{h}_{q} ( When ). The two masks give the spatiotemporal selection 𝐘 q \mathbf{Y}_{q} (Sec.  4.4 ).

Benchmarks. To evaluate avatar referring segmentation, we introduce three benchmarks by augmenting existing human datasets with referring queries and human-annotated targets, following prior evaluations  ( He et al., 2025 ; Li et al., 2025 ) . ZJU-Refer-3D extends ZJU-MoCap  ( Peng et al., 2021 ) with dense part annotations and 20 referring queries covering part names, appearance, wearing relations, and functions. HuMMan-Refer-4D extends HuMMan  ( Cai et al., 2022 ) by converting its fine-grained body-part motion descriptions  ( Zhang et al., 2023 ) into 20 referring queries, while ZJU-Refer-4D extends ZJU-MoCap with 20 queries in the same format. Construction and annotation details appear in Supp. Secs.  B.1 – B.2 .
Metrics. For part segmentation, we report mean per-category IoU (mIoU), overall pixel accuracy (OA), and mean per-category recall (mAcc)  ( Suzuki et al., 2025a ) . For 3D referring segmentation, we report mIoU over queries  ( He et al., 2025 ) . For 4D referring segmentation, vIoU evaluates region accuracy and timing jointly, penalizing both incorrect masks and extra or missed active frames  ( Li et al., 2025 ) . We additionally report tIoU, precision, and recall to assess temporal selection separately  ( Fiebelman et al., 2025 ) . tIoU measures overlap between predicted and annotated active frames. Precision is the fraction of selected frames annotated as active, and recall the fraction of annotated active frames selected (Supp. Sec.  C.1 ).
