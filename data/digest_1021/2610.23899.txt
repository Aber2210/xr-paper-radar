III-E Reconstruction
Our reconstruction consists of COLMAP and 3DGS processes, both adjusted for better accuracy.
III-E 1 Bundle Adjustment
We use COLMAP  [ 26 , 27 ] to refine the camera intrinsics and extrinsics, and to generate a sparse point cloud for initializing the 3DGS optimization.
First, SIFT  [ 19 ] features are extracted and exhaustively matched across all views.
COLMAP’s incremental mapper is then seeded with our initial camera intrinsics and the robot’s reported end-effector poses.
We let COLMAP refine these further, accounting for errors in intrinsics, as well as small, unmodeled pose offsets from the custom microscope mount.
III-E 2 3D Gaussian Splatting
We use the gsplat library’s  [ 34 ] implementation of 3DGS  [ 15 ] .
To suppress floater artifacts while preserving fine structural details, we adjust the default densification hyperparameters, increasing the opacity pruning threshold to τ α = 0.10 \tau_{\alpha}=0.10 and the 2D gradient growth threshold to τ ∇ = 4 × 10 − 4 \tau_{\nabla}=4\times 10^{-4} .

We now experimentally validate the MicroSplat pipeline. First, we ablate the object-centering step to see how well it can localize and keep an object centered during data collection. Second, we test various SfM initialization methods to see which version produces the best results.
We also benchmark against vanilla 3DGS [ 15 ] (trained on images that neither focus-stacked or masked) and three state-of-the-art deblurring approaches in 3DGS: BAGS [ 23 ] , CoCoGaussian [ 18 ] , and Deblurring 3DGS [ 17 ] .
IV-A Runtime
On average, the full MicroSplat pipeline takes 134 minutes per object on a single NVIDIA L40S: 2 minutes for object-centering, 39 minutes for data collection, 33 minutes for pre-processing (focus-stacking and masking), and 60 minutes for the reconstruction (COLMAP and 3DGS).
IV-B 3DGS Training Parameters
We follow the original 3DGS  [ 15 ] training configuration,
optimizing for 30,000 iterations from the COLMAP sparse point cloud with
input images downsampled by a factor of 4 from the microscope’s
1920 × 1080 1920\times 1080 resolution, and use identical settings for both the real and
synthetic datasets.
We deviate from the defaults in two places, both chosen to help with suppressing floater
artifacts: the opacity pruning threshold τ α \tau_{\alpha} is doubled from 0.05 0.05
to 0.10 0.10 , and the 2D
gradient growth threshold τ ∇ \tau_{\nabla} also doubled from 2 × 10 − 4 2\times 10^{-4} to
4 × 10 − 4 4\times 10^{-4} .
