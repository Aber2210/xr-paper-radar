3.7 Training Figure 2 : Overview of EgoRayFormer. A stereo encoder provides image features for a coarse pose proposal and three
refinement layers. Each refinement layer projects shared 3D hypotheses into both views, aggregates their sampled
features, and combines a learned pose update with a regularised ray-intersection update. Causal attention and

EgoRayFormer estimates body pose from a causal stream of stereo fisheye images ( Fig.   2 ). Its decoder uses a shared
3D hypothesis as the unit of stereo feature aggregation: each hypothesis is projected into both calibrated views, and
the paired features support pose refinement. A second path predicts image-plane corrections, intersects the resulting
viewing rays, and blends this geometric update with a learned update. We first describe this decoder, then the encoder,
temporal context and training objective.

4.1 Setup
Datasets.
UnrealEgo2  [ 2 ] is rendered with a synthetic stereo fisheye rig. Its official split has
12,139 training sequences (1,002,671 frames), 1,545 validation sequences
(127,937 frames) and 123,480 test frames. UnrealEgo-RW  [ 2 ] (RW) was captured
with a physical head-mounted rig and has 402 training sequences (79,705 frames), 96 validation sequences (30,083 frames) and 24,012 test frames. As on the leaderboard, RW results are
reported for models trained on RW only and for models trained on UnrealEgo2 and RW; for the latter, the RW model is
initialised with the UnrealEgo2 model of the same seed ( Sec.   3.7 ).
Calibration.
UnrealEgo2 has a single synthetic rig. For RW we keep the intrinsics of the dataset’s calibration files and fit the
camera-from-device transform of every capture session to the device-frame and camera-frame joint annotations of the
training split; validation and test annotations are never used. One test session does not occur in the training data
and uses the rig of the chronologically nearest session.
Protocol and metrics.
Our primary evaluation follows the official device-relative protocol: predictions are the 3D positions of 16 joints
relative to the device centre, with no ground-truth quantity used at inference. We report MPJPE and PA-MPJPE (after
rigid alignment with scale) in mm, using a common scorer based on the public EgoPoseFormer evaluation code.
Ablations additionally report the mean per-joint velocity error MPJVE, the error of frame-to-frame joint displacements
in mm per frame. Every sequence is
