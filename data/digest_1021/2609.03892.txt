4.2 Symbolic Tools for Metric Estimation
Table  1 asks in what form geometry should reach the backbone:
serialized as text for the model to compute over ( Serialized Graph ), or
computed outside and passed as values ( GraFT ). Both are
compared against the Video input on the five VSI-Bench subtasks whose
answers follow from scene geometry, under both perception settings and on two
backbones.
Both Serialized Graph and GraFT improve over the Video input ,
showing that the 3DSG helps in either form; we then isolate the contrast between
the two. First, GraFT is the more accurate on
every setting: on GPT-o3 it improves over Serialized Graph by + 5.9 +5.9 points
under ground-truth perception and + 1.5 +1.5 under realistic perception ,
and on Qwen2.5-VL-7B the gains are larger, + 36.9 +36.9 and + 16.7 +16.7 ; deterministic
functions ( GraFT ) thus outperform in-context arithmetic ( Serialized
Graph ). Second, GraFT makes accuracy far less dependent on the backbone:
under Serialized Graph the weaker Qwen2.5-VL-7B trails GPT-o3 by 33.9 33.9
points on ground-truth perception and 17.2 17.2 on realistic perception ,
but under GraFT it comes within 2.9 2.9 and 2.0 2.0 . By computing the answer
over the 3DSG, GraFT lets a weaker backbone nearly match a stronger one.

4.1 Experimental Setup
Benchmarks. We use VSI-Bench  ( Yang et al. 2025 ) and ScanQA  ( Azuma et al. 2022 ) . The per-module
studies use VSI-Bench’s five metric subtasks for the symbolic tools, the two
directional subtasks of the 422-question subset Struct2D reports
on  ( Zhu et al. 2025 ) for the BEV, and ScanQA for the egocentric module; the
composed system is evaluated on the complete VSI-Bench. Following the official protocols, VSI-Bench numerical tasks are scored by mean relative accuracy and multiple-choice tasks by exact-match accuracy. ScanQA answers are open-ended text, scored by four standard caption metrics, BLEU, METEOR, ROUGE, and CIDEr, which all measure word and phrase overlap with the reference answers (higher is better).
Backbones. GraFT is run on frozen, off-the-shelf backbones with no task-specific training: a frontier closed model (GPT-o3) and an open-weight MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B  ( Bai et al. 2025 ) ) in the per-module studies (Tables  1 – 4 ), joined by Qwen2.5-VL-3B and InternVL2-8B  ( Chen et al. 2024b ) on the complete VSI-Bench (Table  5 ).

GraFT: A Training-Free Framework for Spatial Reasoning in Multimodal Large Language Models via 3D Scene Graphs
Junqing Du
Fernando Ropero

† † thanks: Corresponding author: zhangyanfeng8@huawei.com
Lu Liu We introduced GraFT, a training-free framework that gives a frozen MLLM 3D
spatial reasoning by operating over a single 3DSG. From this 3DSG, GraFT
derives three forms in which the scene’s 3D structure reaches the backbone,
each supplying the evidence its task demands. Per-module studies validate each
capability on its target tasks, and GraFT lifts three open-source backbones by
37% to 65% on VSI-Bench, reaching 51.4 and surpassing
several models fine-tuned for spatial reasoning; even a 3B backbone overtakes
the strongest 72B baseline.
