The estimation of dense hand contact is an emerging area of research, with this study offering a significant contribution through a comprehensive review of publicly available hand-contact datasets up to July 2026. The framework assesses 30 datasets, encompassing around 4 million samples, and amalgamates 18 that meet stringent quality-control standards into a unified training corpus containing 1.16 million samples. The ten excluded datasets failed due to various issues, such as inconsistent or misaligned contact annotations (notably GigaHands, EgoPressure, and HOT3D), domain disparity with bare-hand–object interactions (EgoTouch, InterHand2.6M, and HIC), and absence of real-world imagery (GRAB). To promote reproducibility, the detailed audit is published to assist future researchers in avoiding the burdensome process of dataset screening.

In conclusion, we have developed DenseHap, a comprehensive framework that, in a single forward pass, provides a detailed per-vertex contact, distance, and 3D hand mesh from a single RGB image. After constructing a clean dataset from 28 relevant datasets, we integrated three parameter-independent vertex-query cross-attention trunks into a static WiLoR backbone and trained them using a multiple loss function design to address class imbalance. Our model achieves a throughput of approximately 1.7 × \times that of a reconstruction-contact cascade and improves the F1 score by approximately 10% compared to the current state of the art across zero-shot datasets. .
