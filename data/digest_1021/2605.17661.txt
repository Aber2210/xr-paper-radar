4.1 Datasets We measure trajectory error, reconstruction accuracy, and object-node and scene-graph
quality. The ablations compare sparse depth factors, semantic masking, and temporal
window lengths; the deployment study reports mapping accuracy and processing times.
Standalone dense-prediction benchmarks are reported in the M2H-MX
paper  [ 35 ] .
4.1 Datasets
ScanNet  [ 4 ] is used for system-level trajectory, semantic mesh, and
object-level evaluation; 7-Scenes  [ 29 ] is used for calibrated
trajectory and reconstruction benchmarking; uHumans2  [ 27 ] is used
for dynamic-scene ablations; and the ITC real-world
dataset  [ 33 ] is used for deployment-oriented indoor mapping for M2H-MX-L were measured on an NVIDIA RTX 4080 Super 16GB.
Unless stated otherwise, the benchmark results use sparse depth factors, semantic
masking, and temporal alignment with K = 3 K=3 , the best trajectory setting in the ablation
(Sec.  4.7 ). on representative ScanNet scenes.

Figure 7 : Qualitative semantic mesh comparison on two representative ScanNet scenes. For
each scene, the top row shows the predicted semantic mesh, the predicted labels
transferred onto GT vertices, the GT semantic mesh, and the per-vertex mismatch map;
the bottom row shows zoomed failure regions. Errors concentrate near object boundaries,
thin structures, cluttered furniture regions, and, in scene0233, confusion involving
the broad otherfurniture category.
Figure  7 provides a qualitative view of semantic mesh
performance beyond the aggregate mIoU scores. In both representative scenes, the
predicted mesh preserves the overall room geometry and recovers the dominant semantic
layout with good spatial coherence. Large planar structures such as walls and floors
remain well aligned, and the main furniture arrangement is broadly consistent with the
scene topology. The remaining errors are concentrated around object boundaries, thin
structures, and cluttered furniture areas where partial observations, geometric
fragmentation, or category ambiguity are more pronounced. The zoomed regions indicate
that many failures correspond to semantic leakage between adjacent categories rather
than gross geometric misalignment. In particular, some ambiguous object regions are
absorbed into neighboring dominant classes, and the otherfurniture category is not
preserved reliably as a distinct label, which is especially visible in the scene0233
bottom-row zoom-ins.
