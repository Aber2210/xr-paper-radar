3.1 Problem formulation As illustrated in Figure  2 , V-JEPA Hand reads a raw video clip and
emits, in a single forward pass, the complete bilateral hand trajectory of that
clip together with the camera motion and focal lengths.
The model has two components: a self-supervised video encoder that turns the
clip into a spatio-temporal token grid (Section  3.2 ), and a
query-based trajectory predictor that decodes one output token per frame into
the per-frame state defined below. Section  3.3 describes
our single-stage training with multi-source supervision, combining large-scale
pseudo-labelled video with high-quality data under a shared
training objective.
Figure 2: Overview of V-JEPA Hand. A V-JEPA 2.1 encoder extracts
spatio-temporal features from an egocentric video clip. A ViT predictor jointly
processes these features, one learnable hand-pose query per frame, and an
intrinsics token. The output queries predict bilateral MANO pose, hand
presence probability, and camera motion. The entire clip is
reconstructed in a single forward pass; blue and orange meshes denote the reconstructed
left and right hands, respectively.

4.1 Experimental setup
Datasets.
We evaluate on three egocentric hand benchmarks:
ARCTIC tests reconstruction under severe
occlusion during two-hand object manipulation, while
HOT3D tests robustness to motion blur
from rapid head and hand movements. We additionally evaluate on
EgoStandard , a commercially annotated egocentric dataset spanning
diverse scenes. Its 3D motion annotations are produced by a pipeline that
combines hardware-assisted capture with multi-stage optimization. We exclude
EgoStandard entirely from training and use it to assess generalization to
unseen egocentric data.
The EgoStandard evaluation covers approximately 60 60 minutes of video.
For HOT3D, we follow the HaWoR validation split, covering approximately
49 49 minutes of video.
Table  2 uses the same evaluation splits,
with all world methods rescored on their common true-positive support.
Metrics.
Metrics follow the coverage-aware evaluation by showing where methods miss hands or produce visibly misaligned
meshes.
Figure  4 extends the comparison to challenging in-the-wild
videos, including very low light, outdoor pruning, painting, and household
activities. In the low-light scenes in the first and sixth rows of
Figure  4 , V-JEPA Hand reconstructs a hand that all three baselines
miss. In the outdoor pruning scene (second row), V-JEPA Hand reconstructs both
hands against a background of dense foliage. The painting scene (fourth
row) and the sink scene (eighth row) show hand reconstructions during
object manipulation in cluttered indoor environments.
Across these settings, V-JEPA Hand recovers plausible
hand configurations despite substantial changes in lighting, appearance,
and scene layout. These examples provide qualitative evidence of
generalization beyond laboratory settings.
