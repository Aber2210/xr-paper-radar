### III Method
III-A Semantic Masks Extraction For each semantic class k k and each training frame, we use SAM3 [ 20 ] with text prompts to generate binary masks M k M_{k} . Iris masks are obtained from eye masks by detecting ellipses and warping an annular-shaped template. Masks are made mutually exclusive by prioritizing smaller regions (e.g., iris over eye sclera) to ensure proper layering. SAM3 exhibits strong temporal consistency across frames due to its concept-level understanding; moreover, since our optimization aggregates supervision from hundreds of frames, occasional segmentation noise is naturally absorbed during training. III-B Text-Guided Color Editing To enable intuitive color specification, we propose a two-stage architecture that decouples semantic parsing from color resolution, improving robustness by assigning simpler, well-defined tasks to each component. Semantic Parsing. We fine-tune Flan-T5-Small [ 23 ] to parse natural language instructions into a structured intermediate format. Given input text (e.g., “red matte lipstick”), the model outputs a structured string: region, color, type (e.g., lips, red matte, absolute ). Multiple edits are separated by semicolons. This simplified output space (region names, color descriptors, and edit types) is significantly easier to learn than direct RGB prediction. Color Resolving. The structured output is processed by a deterministic color resolver that handles two types of instructions: Absolute. For absolute color instructions (e.g., “olive skin”), we encode the color description using a pretrained sentence encoder (all-MiniLM-L6-v2) [ 24 , 25 ] and find the closest match via cosine similarity against pre-computed embeddings of region-specific palettes. These palettes contain anatomically plausible colors (e.g., Fitzpatrick scale [ 26 ] tones for skin, natural iris colors based on melanin distributions) and serve as the target color space for each region. Relative. For relative instructions (e.g., “darker”, “more saturated”), we convert the current region color to HSV and apply deterministic modifiers (e.g., reducing V for “darker”, increasing S for “more saturated”).
