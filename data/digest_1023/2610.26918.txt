Table  I reports the quantitative comparison on the real RHD dataset. For within-interval novel-view/temporal interpolation, we compare against 3DGS, Thermal3D-GS, 4D-GS, NTR-Gaussian, and ETGS. For temporal extrapolation, methods that only reconstruct or interpolate thermal appearance within the observed interval are not included, since they do not explicitly provide a continuous thermal-evolution model for future-time prediction under this setting. We therefore compare GTSM with ETGS, which explicitly models continuous thermal evolution on RHD, and additionally introduce a direct MLP baseline to evaluate whether future temperature can be predicted using a generic time-conditioned neural representation.
For the MLP baseline, a shared network directly predicts the temperature of each Gaussian from its spatial position and normalized query time, without introducing explicit thermal states, relaxation dynamics, or temporal rollout. IV-A Dataset Details
GTSM is evaluated on two complementary types of data: a real infrared dataset containing actual thermal evolution and a synthetic dynamic thermal dataset with controllable geometric and thermal variations. The RHD dataset represents the static-geometry setting, where the target geometry remains approximately unchanged while the camera moves and the thermal state evolves over time. The synthetic dataset further introduces time-varying target geometry and is used to evaluate GTSM when both geometry and temperature evolve simultaneously within a 4DGS framework.
Real infrared dataset. The real-data evaluation adopts the Rapid Heat Dynamics (RHD) dataset introduced by ETGS  [ 32 ] . RHD contains real RGB–IR image sequences of multiple objects undergoing practical thermal processes, including warming, cooling, heating, and heat transfer. The sequences are acquired under continuous camera motion with millisecond-level timestamps, providing temporal infrared observations of real thermal evolution. The thermal observations are radiometrically calibrated, and the dataset provides the environmental temperature together with scene-specific temperature bounds corresponding to the infrared grayscale measurements.
The infrared sequences are mainly used to evaluate time-varying thermal-field reconstruction from moving-camera observations and future thermal-state prediction beyond the observed interval. Since the targets in RHD are approximately geometrically static, this dataset corresponds to the 3DGS-based setting described in Sec.  III-D .
Synthetic dynamic thermal dataset. Although RHD provides real thermal observations, datasets simultaneously containing multi-view images, dynamic target motion, and accurate time-varying temperature ground truth remain limited. To evaluate GTSM under dynamic geometry, a thermal-augmented dynamic dataset is therefore constructed based on D-NeRF  [ 33 ] . The original D-NeRF dataset provides multi-view RGB images, camera poses, dynamic target motion, and normalized timestamps. Its camera trajectories and target motion are retained, while corresponding temperature maps are synthesized by assigning simulated component-level temperatures to the foreground target regions.
