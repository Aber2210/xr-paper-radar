Previous methods  [ 2 , 21 , 3 , 20 , 5 ] assume that point clouds to register share the training domain, leading to poor generalizability across domains.
In this work, we break this gap by jointly training a unified point cloud registration model on data from various domains.
To address cross-domain geometric discrepancy, we first design a geometry-aware normalization module which jointly voxelizes and normalizes point clouds into a domain-agnostic standardized geometric space ( Sec.   3.2 ).
RAM then employs a hierarchical registration pipeline  [ 21 , 3 , 20 ] , which first extracts superpoint correspondences and then refine them into dense point correspondences ( Sec.   3.3 ).
Finally, the rigid transformation is estimated by LGR  [ 3 ] . Fig.   2 illustrates the overall pipeline.
Figure 2: Overall pipeline of RAM. Point clouds are first adaptively voxelized and normalized into a standardized geometric space by a geometry-aware normalization module. Aftering extracting hierarchical features, RAM extracts superpoints correspondences with an EGeoTR, and then refines them into dense point correspondences. At last, the transformation is recovered from the correspondences.
3.2 Geometry-aware Normalization
A unified registration model must handle the geometric discrepancy among point clouds from different domains, e.g. , in scene scale and point density. For instance, outdoor LiDAR scans are sparse and long-range (typically ∼ 100 ​ m {\sim}100\text{m} ), while indoor point clouds are denser and short-range (typically < 10 ​ m {<}10\text{m} ). Benchmarks.
Following  [ 13 ] , we evaluate RAM on 12 12 point cloud registration benchmarks, including 4 4 indoor benchmarks: 3DMatch  [ 7 ] , 3DLoMatch  [ 2 ] , ScanNet++  [ 47 ] and TIERS  [ 48 ] , 6 6 outdoor benchmarks: KITTI  [ 9 ] , nuScenes  [ 10 ] , ETH  [ 49 ] , MIT  [ 50 ] , WOD  [ 51 , 52 ] and Oxford  [ 53 ] , and 2 2 object benchmarks: ModelNet  [ 11 ] and ShapeNet  [ 12 ] .
We train RAM on a hybrid set combining 3DMatch, KITTI, nuScenes and ModelNet. Additional details are in the appendix.
Metrics.
Following  [ 3 , 13 ] , we report the results on 4 4 metrics:
(1) Inlier Ratio (IR), the percentage of correspondences whose residuals are below a certain threshold under the ground-truth transformation.
(2) Relative Rotation Error (RRE), the geodesic distance between the estimated and the ground-truth rotations.
(3) Relative Translation Error (RTE), the ℓ 2 \ell_{2} -distance between the estimated and the ground-truth translations.
(4) Registration Recall (RR), the percentage of successfully registered pairs. A pair is successful if its RMSE is below 0.2 0.2 m (3DMatch/3DLoMatch) or if both RRE and RTE are below certain thresholds (other benchmarks).
We adopt stricter RRE and RTE thresholds to enable more rigorous performance assessment.
