5.2 Overall Performance
Table  1 evaluates whether aggressive compression can preserve
both semantic prediction accuracy and RGB rendering quality. Across LERF and
ScanNet-10,
we compare deployable systems from two Language 3DGS paradigms:
direct-lifting methods and render-supervised semantic fields. Semantic metrics
and PSNR measure quality, while storage and peak memory measure deployment cost.
Compared with CF3, the smallest competitor on both benchmarks, ClunoGS achieves
over 5 × 5\times storage reduction while also attaining the lowest peak memory.
It nevertheless ranks second in every semantic and RGB quality metric; its
maximum gaps to LangSplatV2 are only 1.6 mIoU points, 1.1 accuracy points, and
0.07 dB PSNR.
The same trend on both datasets shows that this advantage is not
benchmark-specific.
LangSplatV2 remains the quality leader because its full model retains detailed
semantic coefficients and dictionaries at all three scales. While
direct-lifting methods are designed for efficient querying, both Occam’s LGS
and Dr. Splat trail this render-supervised model in semantic quality. Occam’s
LGS is also storage-intensive on both datasets and incurs the highest peak
memory on LERF.

5.1 Settings
Benchmarks, metrics, and baselines. 5.1 Settings
Benchmarks, metrics, and baselines.
We evaluate on four LERF scenes  [ 17 ] and
ScanNet-10  [ 9 ] , reporting semantic accuracy, RGB PSNR,
storage, and peak memory. Language baselines cover direct-lifting methods
(Occam’s LGS and Dr. Splat  [ 7 , 18 ] ) and
render-supervised semantic fields (LangSplatV2, CCL-LGS, GAGS, and
CF3  [ 22 , 33 , 29 , 20 ] ). Compression
baselines pair three Gaussian sparsifiers (opacity Top- K K , PUP 3D-GS, and
GaussianSpa) with CompGS or C3DGS for attribute
quantization  [ 13 , 40 , 25 , 26 ] .
On LERF , all query relevance maps use the same post-processing and a
fixed 0.4 threshold. mIoU averages query-mask IoU, while localization Acc.
checks whether the peak response lies inside a ground-truth box. On
ScanNet-10 , we follow OpenGaussian’s label protocol and the ten-scene
split used by LUDVIG  [ 37 , 23 ] . Each method
uses its native decoder and scale-selection rule to render ten class-score maps.
We bilinearly sample these scores at projected ScanNet points when the projected
and sensor depths differ by at most 5 cm, average them over valid views, and
take the pointwise argmax. Evaluation includes points observed in at least one
view after applying the same 0.1 activated-opacity threshold to each RGB 3DGS.
Per-scene
mIoU and mAcc average class IoU and recall over the ground-truth classes present
in that scene, followed by an unweighted average over the ten scenes. [ 16 , 41 , 30 ] .
Directly attaching high-dimensional vision-language features to individual
Gaussians incurs substantial storage and rendering
overhead  [ 41 , 30 ] . LangSplat mitigates this
problem using a scene-specific low-dimensional autoencoder, while LangSplatV2
further replaces the heavyweight decoder with sparse per-Gaussian
coefficients over shared dictionaries, enabling efficient high-dimensional
feature splatting  [ 30 , 22 ] . These advances
reduce the cost of semantic feature representation and inference, but do not
target compression of the underlying RGB Gaussian scene. Consequently, a
complete Language 3DGS model still contains the original 3DGS attributes
together with additional semantic parameters. Its compression must therefore
preserve both photometric fidelity and semantic responses.
Standard 3DGS compression often combines sparsification or
pruning  [ 38 , 1 , 40 , 13 , 12 ]
with
quantization  [ 24 , 21 , 25 ] .
Applying them sequentially decouples semantic learning from Gaussian
selection, so RGB-driven sparsification can discard semantically important
Gaussians that subsequent stages cannot recover. Figure  1
illustrates this failure using LangSplatV2  [ 22 ] ,
GaussianSpa, and CompGS: semantic representations degrade whether semantic
learning occurs before or after RGB-driven Gaussian sparsification. Our
unified formulation instead coordinates semantic learning, sparsification,
and quantization throughout optimization.

Language 3DGS under RGB and semantic supervision. Across LERF and ScanNet-10,

RGB and semantic quality. Controlled comparisons show that semantics-aware
selection and primal–dual co-adaptation mitigate the semantic degradation of
sequential compression.
