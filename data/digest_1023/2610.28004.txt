4.5 Real-World Validation We validate our method on two close-range design scenarios for depth-from-defocus and AiF reconstruction, focusing on short range because the defocus cue is less informative further out. The monocular case targets robotic grasping, recovering depth from a single camera over a confined workspace. The stereo case targets macro photography, where a zoomed-in view with a shallow depth of field (DoF) produces strong defocus blur; here a stereo pair adds complementary photometric and geometric depth cues.
4.1 Implementations
Dataset and Environment
We select close-range scenes from MipNeRF 360  [ 2 ] and LLFF  [ 28 ] : 6 scenes ( leaves , flower , fortress , orchids , bonsai , room ) for the monocular case, and 7 scenes (the same 4 LLFF scenes plus counter , kitchen , bonsai from MipNeRF 360) for the stereo case. For each scene, we establish a 3D Gaussian representation with gsplat  [ 47 ] as the virtual environment, then recover metric scale by aligning the reconstruction to metric depth from Depth Anything 3  [ 22 ] . The camera design and task network are jointly optimised across all scenes, with a subset of views per scene withheld for evaluation.
Noise Calibration
We calibrate the noise model on a Basler Dart DaA1280-54uc camera  [ 3 ] (5 ms exposure, 10 dB gain, f/8) following  [ 44 ] . This configuration is treated as the reference point for generalisation.
Optimisation
All camera parameters, coded aperture masks, and task networks are jointly optimised with Adam  [ 19 ] for 20,000 steps with a batch size of 3, using a learning rate of 10 − 2 10^{-2} for the camera parameters and 10 − 3 10^{-3} for the mask and task networks.

Design with 3D Gaussian Scene Representations

Affiliation:  chengyang.yan, donald.dansereau@sydney.edu.au We presented a differentiable framework that jointly optimises camera design and downstream perception tasks, using 3D Gaussian representations reconstructed from real captures as a differentiable virtual environment for image formation. This avoids the scalability limits of derivative-free optimisation. We validated the framework on monocular and stereo depth-from-defocus tasks with jointly optimised coded apertures, sensors, and inter-camera geometry. It consistently outperforms fixed-camera and derivative-free baselines, while revealing interpretable design trade-offs that a decoupled process would miss.
