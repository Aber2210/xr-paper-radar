4.1 Comparison Methods In this section, we present two experimental results using traditional evaluation of neural network alignment and NeRF alignment to evaluate the alignment capability and effectiveness of the proposed method. All experiments were conducted on a computer with an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5090 GPU.
4.1 Comparison Methods
To evaluate the performance of the proposed method, we compared it with the following approaches.
1.
Naive Interpolation: Linear interpolation is performed directly on the weights without any permutation alignment.
2.
Weight Matching [ 1 ] :
The neuron alignment problem is formulated as a linear assignment problem and solved using greedy weight matching.
3.
Progressive Weight Matching:
In contrast to the original weight matching [ 1 ] , which selects layers in random order for optimization, this variant optimizes the permutations sequentially from the input layer toward the output layer.
4.
OT4P [ 12 ] :
This method relaxes the discreteness of permutation matrices and performs optimization in a continuous space using stochastic gradient descent (SGD).
5.
Progressive OT4P [ 12 ] :
A modified version of standard OT4P normally optimizes all layers jointly, where gradient-based optimization is performed sequentially from the input side of the network to the output.
6.
Ours w/o weight matching refinement:
An ablated version of our method that relies solely on correlation-based relaxation and temperature-controlled progressive projection, omitting the final refinement stage.
7.
Ours:
Our complete two-stage approach. It utilizes the continuously aligned solution obtained from the spectral projection as a robust initialization, followed by iterative layer-wise weight matching to achieve highly stable optimization.
For weight matching, random correspondence estimation was performed 10 times using shuffled layer orders covering all layers.
For progressive variants, 100 sequential sweeps were conducted.
For the proposed method, the temperature schedule was set to 𝒯 = { 1.0 , 0.5 , 0.0 } \mathcal{T}=\{1.0,0.5,0.0\} and a single sweep was performed for each temperature value, followed by a final sequential layer-wise refinement.
For OT4P-based optimization, we used AdamW [ 20 ] as the optimizer and terminated the optimization if no improvement was observed for 10 consecutive steps.

Affiliation: The University of Osaka In this paper, we addressed permutation symmetry in Neural Fields and INRs by proposing progressive alignment with temperature-controlled spectral sharpening.
Each layer-wise update is reduced to trace maximization over a correlation matrix. Starting from this continuous representation, we combined a Hungarian anchor with temperature- τ \tau -controlled spectral annealing to converge progressively to a discrete permutation.
This strategy mitigates premature hard decisions in weight matching while avoiding the costly iterative optimization required by gradient-based relaxations, such as OT4P, yielding a deterministic and efficient alignment procedure.
