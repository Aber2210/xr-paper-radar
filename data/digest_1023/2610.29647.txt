V-A Experimental Setup
Datasets.
We evaluate our method on 3 datasets, spanning 33 trajectories across 3 sites, 14 floors, and 2 countries. Sequences are captured on a custom device with synchronized LiDAR and 360° imagery.
The LiDAR points are used only to estimate the ground truth trajectory, which is manually aligned to the floor plan by an expert.
As a VSLAM solution, maps obtained from the equirectangular variant of OpenVSLAM  [ 5 ] are provided.
The floor plans use the semantic class set 𝒞 = { w ​ a ​ l ​ l , w ​ i ​ n ​ d ​ o ​ w , s ​ t ​ a ​ i ​ r ​ s } \mathcal{C}=\{wall,\,window,\,stairs\} , where the corresponding map labels are obtained following Sec.  IV-A .
Tab.  I summarizes the three datasets.
While all datasets are challenging, we order the three sites in increasing difficulty to better assess method performance and limitations.
Site A (Medium) has the the smallest floors (1073.5 m 2 on average) and contains comparatively few distinct rooms per plan.
Site B (Advanced) has larger floors (2750.1 m 2 ) but also a relatively high coverage (88.8%).
Site C (Hard) has the largest floor area (3974.7 m 2 ) and the lowest coverage (28.7%).
In addition, the covered area exhibits structural symmetry, such that multiple candidate alignments can appear plausible to a geometry- or structure-based approach.
