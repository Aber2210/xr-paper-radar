FLoTS-SLAM takes a sequence of monocular RGB images as input and
jointly estimates camera poses and scene geometry.
A pre-trained update operator from DROID-SLAM  [ 27 ] predicts the
dense correspondence target 𝐩 ∗ \mathbf{p}^{*} and its confidence 𝐖 \mathbf{W} .
DINOv2  [ 15 ] extracts the features 𝐟 \mathbf{f} , and
Metric3D  [ 39 ] predicts the depth prior 𝐝 ^ \hat{\mathbf{d}} .
All three networks remain frozen.
In the uncertainty branch (Sec.  III-C ), the per-pixel uncertainty
𝝈 \bm{\sigma} is derived from the residual between the optical flow
and the camera motion induced flow. The estimated uncertainty varies with the
frame alignment accuracy.
In the tracking branch (Sec.  III-D ), the pose is first estimated
from low uncertainty pixels 𝒮 \mathcal{S} with the depths fixed. The high
uncertainty pixels 𝒟 \mathcal{D} are then reprojected using the first-stage
pose before joint refinement. The two branches alternate within each
Gauss–Newton iteration.
III-A System Overview
Fig.  2 illustrates the overall architecture of FLoTS-SLAM.
Given a monocular RGB sequence, our system estimates camera poses and
scene depth for robust tracking and dense reconstruction in dynamic
environments (Sec.  III-B ).
To identify moving objects from motion rather than from appearance,
we derive motion uncertainty from the residual between optical flow
and camera motion induced flow (Sec.  III-C ).
The estimation varies with frame alignment accuracy to reduce false
motion detections caused by misalignment.
For robust tracking, we perform optimization in two stages, both using the
estimated uncertainty (Sec.  III-D ).
The first stage estimates poses from low uncertainty pixels with
depths fixed.
The second stage jointly refines poses and depths, replacing the
correspondence targets of high uncertainty pixels with reprojections
computed using the first-stage poses.
This reduces the influence of object motion on both pose and depth
updates.
