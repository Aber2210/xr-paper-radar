5.1 Setup
Datasets.
We evaluate on the DL3DV  [ 12 ]
benchmark using the standard 4-view protocol, with context frames
selected by the DepthSplat evaluation indices  [ 28 ] .
Our main results use 140 benchmark scenes; each scene provides 4
context views and up to 50 target views for rendering. We evaluate
end-to-end reconstruction on DL3DV because the TokenGS checkpoint
is publicly released only for this dataset. For the pose and scale
analysis (§ 4.1 ), we use a separate set of 100 DL3DV
scenes drawn from the training split, disjoint from the 140 scenes, and additionally 7 scenes from
MipNeRF360  [ 1 ] . Our scale constant is calibrated on
these training scenes and never on the evaluation set. The scale
offset persists on MipNeRF360, indicating the failure modes are
not DL3DV-specific.
Backbone and poses.
We use the publicly released TokenGS
4-view checkpoint  [ 17 ] as a frozen reconstruction backbone
and VGGT-1B  [ 22 ] as a frozen pose estimator. Neither model is
finetuned. Camera poses are obtained either from COLMAP (upper-bound
reference) or from VGGT with our integration fixes.
Pose settings.
We report two settings, both reconstructing
the Gaussians from only the four context views. In the all scene
views setting, VGGT estimates poses from all scene views in a single
forward pass; this validates the composition against COLMAP under
matched information. In the sparse setting, following the
standard pose-free protocol, each target’s pose is obtained from a
forward pass over the four context views together with that target;
the target is used only for its pose, and reconstruction remains sparse.
Metrics.
We report PSNR, SSIM  [ 25 ] , and
LPIPS  [ 30 ] on rendered target views, averaged across all scenes,
and end-to-end inference time on a single RTX 3090 GPU.

【结论/局限】
Why not joint training? We revisited the problem of connecting feed-forward pose estimation to feed-forward 3D reconstruction and showed that the apparent difficulty is not due to pose inaccuracy but to two overlooked, deterministically fixable integration failures: coordinate frame inconsistency and translation scale mismatch. With a convention conversion, all-frame inference, and a simple scale correction—no training, no ground-truth poses, no per-scene optimization—frozen VGGT poses drive a frozen TokenGS backbone to within 0.51 dB of the COLMAP-initialized ceiling at identical inference speed. Our per-camera and cross-dataset analyses establish that rotation error is small and scale offset is systematic, reframing a problem previously attributed to pose quality as one of integration design.
Why not joint training?
A natural alternative is to train VGGT and TokenGS jointly, as in
AnySplat  [ 7 ] and TokenSplat  [ 11 ] . We
deliberately avoid this: joint training is costly and, most
importantly, would obscure the diagnosis. By keeping both models
frozen, we isolate the integration failures and show they are
