Static Scenes.
On NeRF Synthetic, both variants improve quality: d GS-O by +0.46 dB and d GS by +0.53 dB, with rendering speedups of 23% and 15% respectively. On Mip-NeRF 360, efficiency gains are most pronounced: d GS-O achieves 89% faster rendering with +0.78 dB improvement, while d GS achieves 61% faster rendering with +0.97 dB improvement. Notably, d GS-O uses 86% more primitives (2363K vs 1273K) yet remains faster, demonstrating that per-primitive computation cost dominates over primitive count. On 6DGS-PBR, d GS-O shows modest 3% speedup but -0.57 dB quality drop, while d GS recovers and exceeds baseline quality (+0.64 dB).

Datasets.
We evaluate the d -splatting family across five diverse datasets covering both static and dynamic scenes:
•
Static scenes: (1) NeRF Synthetic   ( Mildenhall et al., 2020 ) with 8 synthetic scenes under controlled lighting for evaluating geometric reconstruction; (2) Mip-NeRF 360   ( Barron et al., 2022 ) with 9 challenging unbounded real-world scenes for evaluating view synthesis; and (3) 6DGS-PBR   ( Gao et al., 2025a ) featuring 7 physically-based scenes with complex view-dependent effects.
•
Dynamic scenes: (4) D-NeRF   ( Pumarola et al., 2021 ) with 8 synthetic sequences featuring various deformation types; and (5) 7DGS-PBR   ( Gao et al., 2025b ) containing 6 physically-based dynamic scenes with complex spatio-temporal-angular correlations. compute resources
