3.1 Preliminary & Problem Formulation
We formulate the training of geometric foundation models as a scalable optimization problem, transitioning from a data-scarce supervised regime to a
data-abundant weakly-supervised regime.
3.1.1 Task Instantiation
We focus on unconstrained sparse-view 3D reconstruction .
Definition 3.1 (pose-free sparse-view 3D reconstruction) .
Let f θ : ℐ N → 𝒳 f_{\theta}:\mathcal{I}^{N}\to\mathcal{X} denote a geometric foundation model parameterized by θ \theta . Given a set of unposed RGB images I ∈ ℐ N I\in\mathcal{I}^{N} , the model directly predicts a dense 3D representation 𝒳 \mathcal{X} (specifically, pixel-aligned 3D pointmaps) without requiring ground-truth camera poses.
Our objective is to leverage the abundance of static-scene Internet video to push the generalization limits of f θ f_{\theta} .

The method for calculating the error bars should be explained (closed form formula, call to a library function, bootstrap, etc.)

Datasets We introduce SAGE , a framework that unlocks the scalability of geometric foundation models by exploiting the intrinsic consistency of Internet video.
By formulating adaptation as a regularized weakly-supervised optimization, SAGE balances structural bias (from sparse anchors) and variance reduction (from dense differentiable consistency) to robustly learn from noisy data.
Our experiments show log-linear scaling laws in geometric performance, reducing zero-shot Chamfer Distance by 20–42% on MV-DUSt3R with consistent gains on VGGT.
This work establishes a scalable paradigm for evolving general-purpose 3D models using abundant static-scene video, turning the data scarcity bottleneck into a data abundance opportunity.
